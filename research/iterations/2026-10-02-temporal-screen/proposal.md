# Fixed optimizer clock, shorter recurrent credit assignment

Source-only proposal; no experiment is allocated here. The sole treatment is recurrent-gradient truncation at500 rather than2000 rows. This is a parent-training change on the current ordinary shallow256 GRU, not a new head or inference state.

Both arms process each existing2000-row training chunk as four500-row forward calls. They carry identical numerical hidden states and concatenate all outputs. Candidate detaches hidden state between the four calls; control retains the graph. The immutable trainer computes its original loss once over all2000 rows, then performs exactly one backward, clipping, AdamW, GradScaler, scheduler and EMA update. Incoming state still carries from the preceding outer chunk as in the original recipe. No state is reset at an internal boundary. This achieves globally normalized gradient accumulation without separately normalizing four micro-losses. Teacher EMA uses the same500-row forward partition in both arms; its no-grad carry is numerically unchanged by detachment.

Hypothesis: shorter recurrent credit assignment can regularize the learned representation and improve unseen-chain transfer, even when forward memory is retained. The historical evidence motivates this weakly: E123 found small chunk500 gains; E179 explicitly identified its fourfold optimizer/EMA-clock change; E185 had mixed fold partner effects; E258 supported a chunk500 ensemble member, while E259 exposed substantial class/holdout heterogeneity. These experiments do not establish that the shorter gradient horizon itself helped. The inspected source/ledgers contain no matched500-detach/2000-update experiment. Existing c500 remains an anchor, so redundancy is a serious counterargument.

This differs from closed CTXMEM (frozen16-dimensional event-EMA readouts, half-lives4/32), context-replay (inference-time zero-state interventions with opposite fold effects), and minGRU (different recurrence). It does not claim that2000 rows is too long, or that quiet-row error identifies harmful memory. Serving stays the ordinary GRU with identical tensor layout and recurrence; no extra state, arithmetic or features are deployed.

If selected: same seed3, folds0/1, same211features, two causal h256 teachers, distill-alpha.5, propensity/since weights, auxiliary losses, batch32, four parent epochs with fixed EMAep3. Train candidate and fresh segmented control from identical initialization and sample order. Apply unchanged ordinary H2, two epochs/fixedep1, to each parent. Eight fits and four OOF jobs total. Primary utility is candidate4 versus incumbent student4; matched candidate4 versus segmented-control4 identifies the training treatment; control4 versus incumbent quantifies forward-partition/training drift. Keep the other three anchors and quarter weights fixed. No horizon/phase/seed sweep and no reset readout.

Estimated cost: four parent fits at roughly25–30minutes each plus four ordinary H2/OOF stages, about100–125 GPU-minutes total; two lanes could finish in roughlyone hour. These are extrapolations from the current GRU recipe, not a measured segmented-forward benchmark. Memory need not shrink: concatenated losses retain the total2000-row activation graphs in both arms. First allocated fit can serve as the guarded resource smoke, with no automatic retry or smaller-batch fallback. No inference or native cost experiment is needed to establish unchanged architecture, although any promoted trained checkpoint still needs ordinary export/parity checks.

Falsifier: no useful candidate-versus-control and candidate-versus-incumbent held-chain improvement, especially inconsistent fold/target effects, makes this precise treatment unattractive. Do not turn a null into proof that all temporal learning is exhausted. A candidate/control difference establishes a recipe effect conditioned on one seed and repeatedly exposed folds; it does not identify a universal mechanism or remove adaptive-selection uncertainty. Parent/H2 losses or training gradient differences are not promotion criteria.
