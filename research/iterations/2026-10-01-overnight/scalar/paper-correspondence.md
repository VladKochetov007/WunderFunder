# What SCALARPLE01 does and does not test

Preparation note, 2026-10-01. This note does not change the frozen model, knots, training, timing or promotion gates.

Gorishniy, Rubachev and Babenko study separate numerical-feature embeddings before feature mixing, including piecewise-linear and periodic encodings. Their quantile PLE uses training-feature quantiles and removes zero-width intervals. Their formulation extends the outermost linear pieces beyond the fitted support; MLP inputs concatenate feature embeddings. Their experiments cover tabular benchmarks, not this anonymous streaming competition. [Primary paper, sections 3.1–3.2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.05556v4).

Our adaptation keeps the ordinary input projection and adds a residual before its GELU. For each of44 predetermined raw price-code coordinates, a16-dimensional trainable table is linearly interpolated on up to16 train-only quantile knots. The feature contributions are summed in a fixed order, divided by sqrt(44), and passed through one shared bias-free16→128 projection. The projection starts at zero, leaving the initial ordinary model output unchanged. No statement that these coordinates are decoded physical price levels is required.

For one coordinate with knots k0<…<km and table values t0,…,tm, define a clipped ramp rj(x)=clip((x−kj)/(k[j+1]−kj),0,1). In exact real arithmetic our nodal interpolation equals t0+sum_j (t[j+1]−tj)rj(x). Thus it represents continuous piecewise-linear functions with constant tails. This identity concerns function classes: it does not imply identical floating-point evaluation, initialization, gradients, optimizer regularization or training trajectories under a change of parameterization.

There are three material departures from the paper. First, the new residual clamps outside knot support; the original ordinary path still receives the original value. Second, summing feature embeddings and sharing one16→128 projection restricts all additive embedding contributions to a common at-most16-dimensional subspace before GELU. It is less expressive than independent feature-specific projections after concatenation. Third, this is a residual addition inside a recurrent streaming model under fixed CPU limits, with a fixed two-stage EMA-parent/H2 recipe.

The tested hypothesis is narrow: this inexpensive coordinatewise nonlinear residual might make useful nonlinear functions easier to learn than the ordinary linear input projection followed by the shared nonlinearity. It neither assumes a market microstructure decoder nor proves that GRU lacks the same representational capacity. A negative result would reject this exact residual, initialization, rank, knot resolution and training recipe under the predeclared budget. It would not refute numerical embeddings in general. A positive pooled score alone would not establish the proposed optimization explanation or hidden-domain improvement; the paired controls, both folds, second seed and native-serving gates remain necessary.
