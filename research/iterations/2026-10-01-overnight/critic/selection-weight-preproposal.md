# Selection-weight reframe: independent blind preproposal critique

Written before reading the scout proposal, historical E162 source, or current propensity implementation. This is a specification critique only; no data, nuisance fit, forecast fit or WP query is authorized. The controller's eight forecasting configuration slots are occupied; an explicit amendment would be required before adding this scientific configuration.

Proposed intervention as received: multiply the existing parent weight qX(X)^2 by a bounded ratio qXY(S=1|X,Y)/qX(S=1|X), with selectors fit only on permitted training-side validation chains. No selector, target or mask is supplied at serving.

## What the ratio does and does not mean

If both probabilities are calibrated on the same population and qX is exactly the Y-marginal of qXY, Bayes gives P(Y|X,S=1)/P(Y|X)=qXY/qX. This is a conditional outcome-distribution ratio, not an inverse-propensity correction for missing outcomes. Multiplying qX^2 by the unbounded ratio gives qX*qXY, so the proposed total weight is not automatically the ordinary selected-population risk either. The remaining qX factor is the prior recipe's heuristic emphasis. State the target risk algebra using the actual existing clipping/floors/mask multipliers, not this simplified mnemonic.

Training weights may depend on training targets without causing serving leakage. Legitimacy nevertheless requires that every nuisance fit and any threshold/normalization selection exclude outer-held chains and their outcomes. The training scored indicator is a supervision label, never a serving feature. Prediction masks and known scoring masks are distinct quantities and must not be substituted. A target-aware selector can memorize labels or chain identity; apparent predictability improvement alone does not establish useful or transferable reweighting.

This is not a causal identification claim. Using Y in the selector explicitly models outcome-dependent selection, while the unknown test-period selection mechanism and outcome support remain unverified. Correlation loss, absolute-target metric weighting, label clipping, EMA consistency and auxiliary losses complicate a claim about one well-defined population risk. The exact branch that receives the extra weight must be frozen; multiplying all auxiliaries or teacher consistency would introduce a separate intervention.

## Failure modes to resolve before allocation

1. Existing weights may already include observed S, true-mask floors, qX clipping, a fitted qX power, class weights or target magnitude. A second outcome-aware factor can double-count selection. Trace the actual composed per-row loss weights for train and valid-training sources separately. Require the qXY=qX limit to reproduce the original code bit-exactly.
2. The numerator and denominator must share fitting population, selected-row definition, class/source treatment and calibration. Separately trained probabilities need not obey qX=E[qXY|X]; model-capacity or calibration mismatch can masquerade as outcome dependence. A matched-capacity X-only selector, nested/coherent construction, or explicit ratio-mean calibration check is needed.
3. Nuisance predictions on their own fitting chains can overfit S/Y and generate extreme high-leverage weights. Require inner chain cross-fitting for every nuisance-dependent training row, or make the weaker in-sample design explicit and reject a gain supported only there. No row-random folds, neighbor-chain leakage, alternate outer-fold OOF model, heldfold labels, or empirical fitting on the final evaluation side.
4. Positivity matters. Small qX produces unstable ratios; hard bounds/floors alter the implied target distribution. Freeze one numerator/denominator probability rule, one bounded-ratio rule, normalization and treatment of unsupported bins before readout. Report clipping fractions, effective sample size, maximum chain leverage and ratio distributions by source, class, S and target-magnitude bins. Do not select a bound from an outer WP sweep.
5. Feature/target preprocessing for the nuisance must be fixed. A compressed low-dimensional Y summary may miss relevant selection; an unrestricted selector may memorize it. Decide raw versus clipped targets and whether sign/difference/magnitude are included. The diagnostic must distinguish conditional Y information from merely additional nonlinear X capacity.
6. Training-side validity chains must be exactly the outer-fold allowed set; train-only versus valid-source support may differ. If the nuisance is fitted only on valid data and applied to train data, that is transport across sources and needs a predeclared support diagnostic. An input-only qX cannot justify transporting qXY's Y-dependence.
7. A proper-score gain for predicting S on inner held chains is evidence of selection dependence, not evidence of improved forecasting. A large change in fitted weight distribution can be a failure even if classifier log loss improves. Deployment quality still requires the unchanged baseline comparison with paired seeds and fixed final checkpoints, consuming the normal fit/WP budget.

## Cheapest discriminating evidence to register

First inspect E162 and the old propensity code for duplicate interventions. If the same conditional ratio was already tested, this is a replication unless an explicit new mechanism addresses a documented failure.

A possible pre-forecast diagnostic uses one frozen small set of allowed training-side chains, an inner chain split, and matched X-only versus X+Y selectors. Evaluate held-chain proper scoring loss/calibration and actual bounded-weight stability, reporting all predefined sources/classes/S and Y bins. Fit nothing on held outer chains. Prefer a selector expressive enough to model the frozen Y summary but simple enough to audit, with exactly one regularization choice. A shuffled-Y negative control would need to preserve X dependence or be labeled an imperfect check, not a calibrated conditional randomization test.

A quantitative gate must be proposed before any diagnostic result and must combine reproducible held-chain gain, calibration/ratio coherence, adequate support/ESS, and bounded leverage. The exact numbers need the historical scale and current baseline probability range; they are intentionally not invented here. A diagnostic pass can at most justify asking for a separately registered forecasting allocation. A failed gate, unsupported source transport, or inference that survives only training-chain evaluation closes this fixed proposal; it does not authorize searching target transforms or ratio bounds.

Implementation tests, if later allocated, should include qXY=qX exact baseline equivalence, finite extreme-probability handling, chain-fold exclusion assertions, prefix-causal X construction, no heldfold target reads, explicit loss-branch gradient checks, fixed epoch/no metric-selection behavior, and zero serving changes. Preserve every attempt and distinguish nuisance diagnostics from forecasting results.
