## 1. Test the loss-relevant selection mismatch, not another selector AUC

**Mechanism.** Let X be the information available causally, Y the training target, M the scoring indicator, and c(Y)=clip(Y,-2,2). Scored squared-error risk has expectation

`E[M |c(Y)| (p(X)-c(Y))²] = E[q(X,Y) |c(Y)| (p(X)-c(Y))²]`,

where `q(X,Y)=P(M=1|X,Y)`. This is direct conditional expectation, not an inverse-propensity formula. A causal propensity `q0(X)` can replace q only under the appropriate conditional independence (or an accidental equality of the relevant moments). The current train-source weighting uses causal propensity raised to a power, then other declared weights; true-mask validation training is different. Label-dependent weights are training information only: the delivered predictor still receives X alone. Transfer of q from validation-training chains to train is a strong, unverified assumption.

**Why revisit only the diagnostic?** E-103 used a chain-held-out snapshot GBM: adding Y changed mask AUC .8957→.8979 and failed a +.005 gate. AUC measures ordering of mask probability, not errors in `E[M|Y|Y phi(X)]` or the gradient of the forecast objective. A small global AUC change can coexist with a material change in a rare, high-weight region; it can also be completely useless here. E-170 tested a sharper **causal** propensity on EMA-4 and was null. E-186 tested a coarse causal output residual correction and lost. Those are strong warnings but do not constitute the proposed risk-moment test. The original idea is old; the falsifier and estimand are different. The earlier +.03-scale benefit of correctly weighting scored rows (E-030/033/038) motivates checking this bottleneck, not expecting another gain of that size.

**Cheapest falsifier.** Use only an outer fold's eligible training-side validation chains with known masks. Freeze three disjoint chain-hash partitions (fit/calibrate/audit), class-stratified and capped at 256 chains total, before reading outcomes. Fit one small fixed offset logistic model `logit q = logit q0 + beta·psi(X,Y)`; no architecture sweep. Freeze psi to signed and absolute clipped targets, target contrast, their interaction with causal q0, and class indicators. Cross-fit q0 too: an in-sample propensity cannot be used as an honest audit baseline. Clip probabilities to [.01,.99] in every arm and fit the same calibration method in every arm.

On the untouched-by-the-selector audit chains, use one immutable parent predictor and its last-head activation phi(X) (including the intercept). A single causal forward pass extracts p and phi; there are no predictor updates. The PRIMARY comparison is the normalized last-layer weighted-MSE gradient for each target:

`g(q) = 2 sum[q |c| (p-c) phi] / sum[q |c|]`, compared with `g(M)`.

Compare `||g(qXY)-g(M)||` with `||g(qX)-g(M)||` and the shuffled-target control, plus cosine to g(M). Freeze normalization of these discrepancies from the fit partition, and include a scale floor so a near-zero true-mask gradient cannot produce an artificial relative improvement. This is a concrete forecast-loss-gradient comparison, not a selector accuracy score. It uses a frozen, possibly training-fitted predictor; the result is a training-objective diagnostic, not independent generalization evidence. The parent checkpoint and all partitions must be frozen before this pass. Secondary reconstruction checks measure discrepancies from actual-mask weighted moments, separately for each target: `M|c|`, `M|c|c`, `M|c|c²`, and `M|c|c phi(X)`. For these secondary checks, use 16 fixed signed projections of the same head activation, with exact signs and training-only scales recorded before fitting. Also report class and activity blocks, effective weight sample size, and train/valid support overlap. This is a loss-relevant surrogate, not a WP query. Its scale/normalization must be fixed from the fit partition; otherwise a researcher can hide a failed moment by changing units. Negative control: same parameter count and target marginals but Y supplied from a deterministic different chain of the same class; preserve whole contiguous blocks, not independent row shuffles.

Proposed spending gate: at least 20% lower gradient-discrepancy norm than both causal and shuffled controls for BOTH targets on both predetermined audit halves, with the causal control's initial discrepancy exceeding 1% of the frozen gradient scale. Require positive cosine to the true-mask gradient whenever its norm exceeds the frozen scale floor. Require effective sample size at least 80% of the causal control and no class's moment error more than 10% worse. Fail or insufficient support means **no forecast fit**, even if AUC improves. These numerical cutoffs are pragmatic, unvalidated resource gates. A pass only justifies an experiment: it does not establish forecast relevance or transport to hidden test.

**Matched forecast test if allocated.** Same H128 initial weights, parent-four-epoch/EMA-ep3 and ordinary two-epoch H2/ep1, same rows, seeds, optimizer, auxiliaries and existing since weights. Change only the precomputed train-source mask factor: same-calibrated causal q(X) versus q(X,Y), not an arbitrary new exponent selected on OOF. Keep true-mask training rows identical. The risk mechanism requires a matched causal-factor control; a third unchanged production-weight arm is needed to establish practical utility if the causal control differs from the incumbent. Seeds 3/4 and folds 0/1, fixed endpoints; no head-only shortcut can test the trunk representation claim.

**Cost and risk.** Initial selector fitting roughly 5–15 CPU minutes and <4 GiB if features are streamed, plus a capped one-GPU parent forward pass to extract the fixed head activations (roughly 5–10 minutes, <=5.5 GiB); estimates, not measured. Full test is 12 parents plus 12 H2 stages with three arms, roughly 3–5 A4000 GPU-hours from historical 13–15-minute parents; do not launch this entire cost by default. No serving overhead or new native code. Leakage risks are q0's fit provenance, using labels outside outer training folds, chain sharing, and selecting psi after audit results. Distribution transport and the narrow E-103 gain are the biggest scientific risks. This ranks first because an inexpensive negative result can close a consequential misspecification argument correctly.

## 2. Random-origin training with ordinary inference, conditional on a real origin sensitivity

**Mechanism.** Dataset segments begin at an artificial boundary. A recurrent model and its handcrafted event counters may learn a response to that particular history origin. Training on additional causal origins could teach faster recovery and reduce dependence on incidental pre-segment context, while retaining ordinary uninterrupted state at deployment. This is robustness to missing history, not a claim that long history is harmful.

**What is and is not tested.** R1 reset the GRU every 2,000/4,000 rows in **both training and prediction** and lost; its ordinary score was already fairly flat along sequence position. E-161's burn-in/position and E-211's warm-up-summary correction were also negative. Single-sequence chains being harder is confounded by their much larger class-3 share; it is not proof of a cold-start gap. CURRENT_STATE explicitly lists training-only random-origin augmentation as untested. Thus this has a weaker prior than its intuitive story suggests, and must first earn a fit.

**Cheapest falsifier.** On a fixed training-only chain manifest, compare unchanged-model predictions for the same suffix under (a) the available original causal prefix and (b) a fresh causal stream starting at one fixed hash-selected internal offset per sequence. Rebuild **all** event counters, EMA features, normalization state and GRU state from the new origin; resetting only h while retaining full-prefix engineered features does not test the mechanism. Exclude the first 99 new-origin rows, and report the first 1,000 remaining rows separately from the rest. No future rows may initialize state. Block by class/activity and compare equal target rows, not easy and hard sequence populations.

Use training-side weighted squared error, not exposed OOF WP. Proposed gate: at least 1% relative error increase in the cold-origin version on the first 1,000 admitted rows, present in at least three classes and in both fixed chain halves; later error need not increase. Record the absolute scored-weight share of that region. If the effect is negligible or confined to one already-known hard class, do not fit. A positive sensitivity is necessary evidence for this intervention, not proof that augmentation will improve ordinary inference. Fresh origins remove real information, so loss worsening may be unavoidable rather than remediable.

**Exact candidate/control direction.** First candidate uses one prehashed internal reset boundary per training sequence/epoch, drawn from chunk-aligned positions 2,000…16,000. Reset both feature and recurrent states, and exclude the next 99 rows from every objective. The matched control keeps its original state but masks exactly the same loss rows. Both see the same row order, labels, optimizer steps and retained loss positions; candidate and control use identical ordinary inference at evaluation. No periodic inference reset, no context-length search, no change of half-lives. A plain-production anchor remains necessary because the matched masking control itself differs slightly. Start with parent learning, not another frozen-head fit; two seeds and both folds for a scientific replication, fixed final checkpoints.

**Cost and risk.** The diagnostic is a bounded partial forward pass, approximately 5–15 minutes on one GPU for a capped manifest, not a full evaluation. Training has near-zero FLOP growth if alternate causal feature sequences are cached once; its main risk is engineering exact causal resets and auxiliary-label boundaries. Serving is unchanged. Prevent label construction from crossing an artificial end/reset unless the task definition explicitly permits that target as supervision; document the decision rather than silently changing only main-loss masks. This direction changes data coverage rather than adding capacity, and its natural target is robustness, not a promised local WP jump.

## 3. Test gradient horizon before buying a new long-memory architecture

**Mechanism.** `scripts/train_rnn.py` carries the GRU state forward but detaches it after every chunk (default 2,000 rows). Thus forward history can be long while credit assignment is truncated. The question is whether earlier inputs affect useful later errors through learnable state dynamics that the truncated gradient omits. This differs from root's state-refresh proposal: refresh changes the parameter age of the incoming state; this changes which earlier computations receive gradients. It also differs from frozen CTX half-life summaries, which did not train the backbone to preserve new information.

**Counterevidence must constrain the story.** Two thousand rows is already long relative to many observed event/target horizons. Longer chunk/c500, long causal drift features, dual clocks, event attention, and the segment Transformer have not demonstrated a broad missing long-context signal. Large residual autocorrelation mostly reflects overlapping future outcomes (E-238), and must not be used as evidence for predictable memory. No paper result on text or synthetic memory establishes a benefit on these inputs.

**Cheapest falsifier.** One fixed current parent checkpoint, 32 predeclared training-only 8,000-row windows; no optimizer updates, no loss/epoch selection. Compute the loss only on the last 2,000 rows. With weights, forward inputs, state initialization and AMP policy held identical, compare full-window gradient g8 with a gradient g2 whose graph is detached at row6,000. Outputs and losses must match before gradients are interpreted. Separately report `||g8-g2||/||g8||`, cosine, and the component norms for encoder, lower/upper GRU and head. The head-gradient difference should be numerical zero: it is a useful implementation negative control. Use generated finite-difference checks before real windows.

Proposed gate: relative omitted-gradient norm >=10% in the median window and >=5% in at least three quarters of windows, with consistent nonzero recurrent/encoder components on both fixed chain halves. Reject this *current-GRU truncation rationale* if the tail is negligible; it does not prove that every imaginable memory architecture is futile. Gradients from one trained checkpoint can miss features the current network has learned to forget; note that limitation rather than silently expanding the gate.

**Matched training if allocated.** Same H128 network, 8,000-row macroblocks, one optimizer/EMA update per macroblock in both arms. Control detaches at each 2,000-row sub-boundary and accumulates four subloss gradients; candidate preserves the graph through all four subchunks. Both weight the same four losses identically and use an identical scheduler in macroblock units. Activation checkpointing/microbatch accumulation can bound memory; reductions and AMP need independent tests. An ordinary 2,000-row-update reference distinguishes changing update cadence from extending credit assignment. The first treatment therefore remains native-compatible and costs **nothing at serving**. Only if this training bottleneck is supported should an independently specified stable diagonal state-space student be considered to make long-gradient training cheaper; that architecture is not automatically authorized by this card.

**Cost and risk.** Gradient audit plausibly 5–20 minutes GPU, <5.5 GiB by serial windows/checkpointing; it requires a generated resource smoke, not assumptions from smaller models. A complete three-arm/two-seed/two-fold parent experiment could consume several GPU-hours and the longer-BPTT candidate could cost 2–4× ordinary training. Do not start it as a casual short fit. Inference remains unchanged; data leakage is avoided by training-only windows and an immutable checkpoint. A gradient difference can be pure optimization noise, so only an eventual paired held-chain forecast test decides utility.

## Why these, and what I would not run next

The strongest immediate action is a small **loss-relevant selection-gradient diagnostic** or the **causal-origin diagnostic**, not more frozen-head features. Both can falsify their motivating story cheaply and preserve the fast student. The long-gradient audit is the more structural reserve; its prior is weaker because current chunks are already long.

I would not immediately run generic predictive-state pretraining: E-232 correctly says existing sign-head readout does not test it, but E-251 already supplied high-fidelity future quote-path targets (target-contrast correlations about .93–.96) and lost materially. Next-code predictability and the quant transition entropy gain are not sufficient price relevance. I would also not revive conv16 from its attractive two-epoch result: E-181's later EMA recipe refuted it. A repair of the old per-chunk convolution boundary would not erase that negative test; E-175 already compared streaming and chunk resets and found very small score differences.

For any promoted experiment, use a fixed exact ensemble or replacement-slot readout, both fold signs, and paired independent seeds. A proposed practical spending threshold is +.0005 pooled WP with positive fold deltas in each seed; it is deliberately a nontrivial competition gain, not a p-value. Report lower outcomes rather than making a fresh blend. All existing validation folds are exposed, so this remains discovery evidence. Rehashing those chains into a new split cannot create independent confirmation; even a positive full local replication will not guarantee hidden-test gain. No inference-time timing adaptation is acceptable under the repeated-prediction determinism rule.

Primary literature supports only the general mechanisms: conditional reweighting addresses sample selection under explicit assumptions ([Cortes et al., Sample Selection Bias Correction Theory](https://research.google/pubs/sample-selection-bias-correction-theory/)); truncated BPTT trades memory for gradient bias ([Aicher, Foti and Fox, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07473)). Stable state-space layers can use parallel scans during training ([Smith, Warrington and Linderman, S5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04933)); this is a feasibility reference, not a recommendation to copy their benchmark recipe or evidence of forecast improvement here. The task-specific recommendations and thresholds above are my inference from the local record.

**Requested architecture backup, not a fourth allocated card.** minGRU is an old *unexecuted proposal* in `research/lit/opus_arch.md:A3` and `research/panel/ml_lead.md:X8`; I found no matching implementation or empirical entry in the inspected `src`, `scripts`, `native`, experiment and hypothesis ledgers. Its case should be width per serving cost, not generic long memory: input-only gates make the state update elementwise, removing dense hidden-to-hidden matrices, while dense input projections still cost O(DH) (O(H²) when D=H). [Feng et al., primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01201v3) support the architecture and parallel-scan training, not the old local proposal's unmeasured latency or accuracy predictions. A wider minGRU student with the same causal teacher/objective could therefore be a distinct fallback if shallow-wide GRU distillation fails; it has a weaker empirical prior and higher implementation risk than the shallow256 route because it removes state-dependent gating and needs independent sequential/scan/AMP/native parity. The cheapest initial test is a generated exact-equation streaming/parallel parity and resource prototype, followed only if affordable by a fixed same-teacher paired forecast comparison at a measured serving budget. Do not treat reduced MAC count as a runtime pass or its untested status as novelty.
