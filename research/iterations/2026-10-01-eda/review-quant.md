# Independent review of QMARK-EDA-01

Reviewer: event-clock branch; inspected the frozen `quant-mark-plan.json`, `quant_mark_eda.py`, and its deterministic self-test implementation before seeing QMARK empirical outcomes. No additional data probe was run for this review. **No blocking implementation defect found.**

The vectorized `ranks_before` calculation agrees with prequential prediction. At mark n, the frequency numerator counts occurrences strictly before n; the transition numerator counts earlier completed `(previous,current)` pairs, and its denominator counts previously completed uses of the current previous-mark context. The current mark indexes the probability being scored but does not enter the predictive distribution before scoring. The simple online loop, prefix comparisons and suffix-mutation self-tests directly attack this distinction. The first mark uses the global prior; all state resets at the next sequence. Priors and medians come only from the opposite diagnostic half.

The adaptive-frequency F control is essential and implemented: a transition model beating only a global G prior could merely adapt to sequence-specific mixtures. Here T must improve on the causal F estimate, with fixed smoothing. T and F have different effective context counts and different priors, so their comparison establishes predictive usefulness of this complete frozen transition recipe, not an isolated causal contribution of adjacency alone.

Full shuffle preserves each retained sequence's mark counts; block128 shuffle additionally preserves each contiguous block's counts. The same priors, predictor state resets, fixed permutation seeds and mark limits apply to all conditions. Three null seeds are reported rather than selected. Whole-chain resampling is paired across conditions and stratified by diagnostic half and metadata class. There is one sequence per selected chain, so the code's chain record is the intended resampling unit. The intervals condition on fitted centers, priors, detector, selected sample and null seeds; they omit nuisance-fit variability, selection and transfer uncertainty. They are descriptive rather than calibrated randomization tests.

The central interpretation risk is **detector-induced serial dependence**. The emitted sequence is defined by an immediate raw-code change plus nonneutrality and validity filters. Such observation rules can themselves induce persistence, alternation or reset patterns. A shuffle of coarse marks need not be realizable by a raw stream satisfying the same detector; block128 preserves frequencies, not this observation mechanism. Consequently a positive original-minus-null advantage can establish predictability of the detected code process, but cannot establish a latent trade-execution process, signed order flow, or forecastable prices. The plan already disclaims these claims; preserve that distinction when reporting an interesting result.

The first4096-emitted-mark cap preserves causal event order but emphasizes early portions of event-rich sequences. The first16/after16 split and cap-retention counts should remain visible. Equal-chain and event-weighted losses answer different questions; neither estimates the competition's absolute-target-weighted scoring utility. Target-derived class is only a sampling/reporting label and is absent from predictors.

Minor reporting completion: the script saves detected-before/after-cap counts and bit-tie counts rather than every requested ratio explicitly. The memo can derive cap retention and bit-tie fractions from these frozen counts with their exact denominators; this requires no new diagnostic or model selection. Report empty/one-event sequences and undefined MI/gain denominators rather than silently treating them as zeros.

Potential future discriminator, **not authorized in this round**: a detector-respecting raw-code surrogate or a predictive control based solely on mechanically imposed reset/hold constraints could separate observation artifacts from richer sequential structure. The present block null alone cannot do that. This does not invalidate the registered descriptive mark-predictability test.

## Outcome review after the frozen run

The primary registered result is strong **for this observable-code prediction task**:256 chains and681,335 retained marks, mean per-chain log-loss gain F−T **0.78976 nats/mark**, conditional chain95% interval **[0.78148,0.79799]**. Both directions are positive (+0.79236,+0.78715); all four descriptive classes are positive (+0.72522,+0.82806,+0.94058,+0.66517). Event-weighted gain0.78637 is close to the equal-chain result. Thus this is not solely one half, a selected market class, or many events from a few chains dominating the chosen estimand. The registered nuisance-feature nomination gate passes. It still does not allocate a price model or establish competition benefit.

The null comparison requires care. Mean full-shuffle gain is approximately**−0.10331**, and block128-shuffle gain**−0.08541**. Original-minus-block is0.87516, interval[0.86434,0.88684]. Because each surrogate retains the transition prior R learned on *original-order* fit streams, this contrast includes distribution mismatch of that prior and the finite-sample cost of splitting adaptive counts among contexts. It is not an isolated estimate of causal order-flow information. Re-fitting priors under each null would be a different future study; it must not be introduced now to polish the result. The original T-versus-F comparison remains valid as a comparison of the two fully specified causal online estimators.

A useful algebraic check explains another result: the adaptive-frequency predictor is exchangeable in its **total** sequence likelihood. With counts n_j and total N,

`log P_F(sequence) = log Γ(κ) − log Γ(N+κ) + Σ_j[log Γ(n_j+κπ_j) − log Γ(κπ_j)]`.

Therefore both full and block shuffles leave its complete-sequence log loss unchanged when they preserve counts. That is exactly visible: F remains1.92527635 across every surrogate, while G is also unchanged. It does not mean F was accidentally evaluated noncausally: the online probabilities vary with prefixes, but their product telescopes to a count-only expression. The null tests alter the transition estimator's context statistics and mismatch to R, while F's total is invariant by construction.

Original same-mark adjacency is5.79%, versus approximately18.11% after full shuffle and20.12% after block shuffle. This supports an anti-persistent encoded process under the registered detector/encoding. It does **not** identify alternating trade sides or actual executions: current-code-change selection, omitted neutral rows, transforms and sensor/reset conventions can create such structure. The lag1 mutual information is descriptive and finite-sample biased; the cross-fitted predictive loss is the more defensible empirical result here.

Conclusion for the controller: retain “past emitted code marks strongly predict the next code mark beyond this adaptive-frequency baseline.” Reject the stronger labels “order-flow alpha,” “forecastable future price,” “signed aggressor flow,” or “causal trade process” on this evidence. Any next feature study must separately establish incremental information beyond the existing model/features and relevance to the fixed serving ensemble, with detector-respecting nuisance controls if a microstructure interpretation is sought.
