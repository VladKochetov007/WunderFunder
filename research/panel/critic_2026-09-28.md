# Critic report, 2026-09-28: keep_k4 vs incumbent, deployment audit

Independent critic. I wrote my own evaluation code and did not reuse `scripts/decide.py` or any `research/` numbers. My scripts and raw outputs are in `research/panel/critic_2026-09-28/`. Unless a line says otherwise, every number below is the pooled global clipped |y|-weighted Pearson on scored rows, averaged over t0 and t1. SDs come from a chain bootstrap (2000 resamples of `cache/chain_folds.npz` chains inside the evaluated set). "inc" is the equal blend of `ab67_final_since_s{4,3}_gcorr`.

## Verdict

keep_k4 is somewhat more likely than not to beat the incumbent on an unseen period. My estimate is P ≈ 0.6 under the global metric. The expected gain is +0.0000 to +0.0006, which the public leaderboard cannot resolve (about ±0.0006 per paired comparison). No proxy shows a significant loss. The bigger risk is operational: at four members the runtime governor sits within about 3% of its trigger. It then silently changes the member set, and the result is not reproducible run to run. It also gives no protection if the private dataset has more rows than 39.4M.

I found no causality leak, reset bug, or cross-sequence data flow in the Rust or Python paths. The governor is the only cross-sequence state.

### Where the two proxies disagree

The disagreement comes from the models, not from which sequences are sampled. 189 holdout sequences lie in folds 0 and 1, so both model sets have them out of sample. On those same sequences, the fold lever is +0.00207 ± 0.00083 and the final lever is +0.00006 ± 0.00082. The difference-in-differences is +0.0020 ± 0.0011.

Contamination does not explain it. Neighbour leakage would make the holdout behave partly like in-sample data, and in-sample the k4 lever is large (+0.0026 in both the final and the fold setups). So contamination would push the holdout toward k4, not toward zero.

What does differ is prediction scale. On the holdout, the final k4 blend shrinks class-3 predictions relative to the incumbent (t0 prediction SD ratio 0.965, versus 0.984–0.988 for classes 0–2). Class 3 carries 46% of the pooled weight. In the fold analogs the ratios are all about 1.0. A pooled metric is sensitive to this between-class calibration even when within-sequence skill improves.

### Checks on proxy hygiene

- **Fold incumbent analog uses a different recipe.** The fold analog pairs `since4s3` (since_weight 32) with `base4s4` (since_weight 0). The real incumbent is two since-weighted models. The lever reproduces on since-only bases (table above), so the conclusion survives the mismatch.
- **No holdout checkpoint selection for these members.** `train_rnn.py` saves `model.pt` whenever the holdout score improves. I checked: `ab67_final_since_s{4,3}/model.pt` equals `model_ep3.pt`, the last epoch. The ab84 members start from a fixed `@ep3`. The holdout has still been used for many earlier recipe decisions, so a generic winner's-curse tilt toward the incumbent recipe is plausible but cannot be measured.

### Metric sensitivity

The repo's own `README.md`, `METRIC.md`, `docs/faq.md` and `utils.py` describe a per-sequence averaged metric, while the brief and `src/wnn/gmetric.py` say global pooled. Under the per-sequence metric the two proxies agree:

- holdout: +0.00146 ± 0.00050
- folds: +0.00145 ± 0.00030

I also scored the six previously uploaded packages on the holdout under both metrics and compared with their public scores:

- **Rank order:** Spearman 0.94 under both metrics.
- **Pairwise deltas:** correlation with public deltas is 0.80 (global) and 0.86 (per-sequence).
- **The mixed-EMA6 ZIP:** global −0.00002, per-sequence −0.0026, public −0.0006.
- **Implied public delta for keep_k4:** +0.00001 on the global fit, +0.0006 on the per-sequence fit.

These six points cannot decide which metric the leaderboard uses.

### Which proxy I believe

For the level of the gain on these specific weights, I believe the global holdout. It measures the deployed weights, and the paired comparison on the 189 shared sequences shows the fold models do not stand in for them. I treat the fold result as a real recipe-level prior: it is class-uniform, robust to the base pair, and matches the per-sequence holdout. Combining both gives an expected global delta of about +0.0003 ± 0.0005 before public shrinkage (historically about 0.5×). Hence P ≈ 0.6. If the scorer is per-sequence, or behaves like it, P is about 0.8.

### Routed variant

Routing uses the true class. On the holdout, routed − inc is +0.00006 ± 0.00069 and routed − k4 is +0.00004 ± 0.00026. In the folds, routed − k4 is +0.00069 ± 0.00008, but routing was chosen after looking at these same folds, so that number is optimistic.

## 2. Deployment audit (`solution_ensemble.py`, `solution_routed.py`, `native/src/python.rs`)

### Checks that passed (local v3 build, governor disabled with `WNN_BUDGET_S=1e9`)

- **Parity with cached batch predictions.** keep_k4: corr ≥ 0.99979 on 3 holdout sequences. routed_k4_all: corr ≥ 0.99989. The routed package picked the right group for classes 0, 2 and 3. Per-member differences in per-sequence WP are about ±9e-4 with random sign, which looks like batch-precision noise rather than a porting bug.
- **Reset.** A fresh model and the same model after two other sequences give bit-identical output.
- **Causality.** Perturbing rows ≥ 5000 leaves rows < 5000 bit-identical.
- **Unusual inputs.** All of these give finite outputs of shape (2,):
  - NaN and inf rows, including an all-NaN first row
  - `need_prediction` set on row 0
  - 40-row and 10-row sequences
  - float64 `state`
- **Returned arrays are not aliased.**
- **Packages match what was tested.** Zip contents match `pkg/` by sha256, and each zip's `solution.py` equals the `submission/` file.
- **Python version.** The abi3-py311 build is fine: the incumbent used the same native abi3 build and scored, even though `README.md` says Python 3.10.

### Findings

1. **The governor is cross-sequence state that changes predictions.** It carries only wall time and row counts, not data values. Step time is only weakly data-dependent (the `xj != 0.0` skip), so it is not a data leak and not test-time learning. But when it fires, a sequence's output depends on how fast earlier sequences ran. That breaks the FAQ rule "repeated runs must produce identical predictions", and a strict code review could call it state across sequences.

   I saw this locally. On this loaded machine (186 µs/row against 79.2 allowed), the governor dropped from 4 members to 1 at the start of the third sequence. That sequence's output then differed from a fresh run by up to 0.53, with corr 0.97–0.98 against batch.

2. **The governor sits on a knife edge.** The trigger is 52 min / 39.4M rows = 79.2 µs/row. My K4 figure of about 76.9 µs/row is an assumption taken from the repo's ~18 µs/member scorer estimate plus onorm's extra cost, not a measurement. Simulating the actual `_govern` logic (`govsim.py`):
   - With 3% per-sequence timing noise, P(some member is dropped) = 0.17.
   - On a machine 3% slower, a drop to K3 is certain, even though K4 would finish in about 52 of the 60 minutes.

   The first decision is based on a single 20k-row sequence, and dropped members are never added back.

3. **`EXPECTED_ROWS` is hard-coded to 39.4M.** Once `rows ≥ EXPECTED_ROWS` the governor turns itself off. If the private "Final Dataset" has 1.2× as many rows, K4 takes about 60.7 min with no drop, which is a timeout. K2 would take about 31 min.

4. **The budget clock starts at the first `predict` call.** Imports, weight loading and any harness pre-reading are not counted. That time comes out of the 8-minute slack.

5. **Robustness gaps (low probability).**
   - A NaN first row is sanitised to 0. At row 1 the deltas and the held i0 deltas then become full price levels until the next i0 price event, and `OnlineNorm` keeps the zero row for the whole sequence.
   - Inputs around 1e20 produce outputs around 1e20. They stay finite and the metric clips them.
   - In `OnlineNorm`, float32 `sumsq` overflows for |x| ≳ 1.8e19. That column's z-score then stays at 0 for the rest of the sequence.
   - A non-finite ensemble output is zeroed, but the recurrent state is not reset.
   - `panic = "abort"` means any Rust panic kills the run. I found no reachable panic.

6. **Routed-specific.**
   - Misroutes: 4/1873 for the `probe_routed` detector and 5/1873 for the `final_routed_k4_all` detector. Both got 0/368 wrong on the holdout. The minimum top-2 margin is 0.055 for the `_all` detector.
   - Row 0's softmax is included in the class vote. This is a quirk and harmless.
   - Shared members w2 and w3 are stepped twice during warm-up, about 0.5% extra cost.
   - During warm-up the governor's per-member cost estimate spreads the time of 8 member-steps plus the detector over k_max = 4. This is minor.

## 3. Top 3 risks and executable checks

1. **Governor or runtime makes the scored member set nondeterministic, and gives no protection against a larger private set.**
   - Ran: `critic_2026-09-28/govsim.py`, and `check_pkg.py … keep --governor` (shows the 4→1 drop and the resulting divergence).
   - Not run (decisive): time `final_keep_k4` in the scorer image with 1 vCPU and `WNN_BUDGET_S=1e9` over at least 400k rows, and compare µs/row with 79.2.
   - Mitigation: either ship a fixed member count after that measurement, or require at least a 5-sequence window and a budget above the nominal K4 time with margin.

2. **The keep_k4 decision depends on the proxy and metric.** The global holdout shows a tie; the per-sequence holdout and the folds show about +0.001 to +0.0015.
   - Ran: `analyze.py`, `extra.py`, `perseq.py`, `metric_vs_public.py`.
   - Pre-register: if keep_k4 scores ≤ 0.6622 on public, downweight the fold and per-sequence proxies for this model family.

3. **The routed variant adds complexity for no holdout gain.** Its fold gain was chosen after looking at the folds, and the detector misroutes 0.2–0.3% of validation sequences in-sample.
   - Ran: `check_detector.py` (both detectors) and the routed rows of `analyze.py`.
