# Activations, optimizer interactions, and transfer

User-specified [Mining Generalizable Activation Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05688), February2026, searches small synthetic tasks with distribution shifts and then checks downstream tasks. It reports pointwise GELU-plus-sine and GELU-times-sinc functions; the reported comparisons vary by task. Its batch-statistic variants overfit its small laboratory. For causal streaming inference, use the pointwise functions and keep training/evaluation output invariant to future rows and unrelated batch members. This paper does not establish gains for this competition.

First paper-inspired definitions: GELU(x)+0.1sin(x), and GELU(x)*(1+0.5sinc(x)), where sinc is normalized sin(pi*x)/(pi*x), including its finite limit at zero. Our cosine residual subtracts1 so the added term vanishes at zero. It is an additional experiment, not a function claimed by that paper. Plot activation/derivative ranges and actual preactivation occupancy before assigning a periodic mechanism.

[On the Synergy of Optimizers and Activation Functions: A CNN Benchmarking Study](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/13/13/2088), 2025, compares optimizer–activation combinations on three image datasets. This supports measuring the interaction rather than inheriting a universal activation ranking; image-classification findings are not recurrent weighted-correlation evidence. The publisher search excerpt was available; the full publisher page fetch failed, so detailed methodological claims remain unverified.

[Descending through a Crowded Valley](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/schmidt21a.html), ICML2021, benchmarks15 optimizers across more than50,000 runs. No optimizer universally wins; choices and hyperparameter tuning depend on workloads. A single default-LR pilot here compares fixed recipes, not optimizer families at equal tuning budget. Later schedule/learning-rate experiments require equal budgets and preregistered settings.

[Neural Networks Fail to Learn Periodic Functions and How to Fix It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08195), 2020, motivates a periodic inductive bias. Anonymous feature codes and nonstationary labels do not guarantee such periodicity. A sinusoidal residual may help through smooth local gradients without learning a real market period.

[Three Decades of Activations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09092), 2024, catalogs400 functions. It is a taxonomy and motivation to inspect redundancy, not a comprehensive controlled ranking. [SG-Blend](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23942), 2025, studies learned interpolation between smooth activations; that motivates a separately budgeted learned mixture after fixed-mixture controls.

First fixed matrix: eight activations × AdamW/MuonAdamW, all16 fits, no pruning from old negatives. Change embedding and residual-head activations together initially; keep internal GRU sigmoid/tanh gates unchanged for stability. Stage2 must localize embedding versus head and then separately test dropout, residual amplitude, and constant versus warmup/cosine schedules. Maintain matched widths/parameter counts, initialization and minibatch order; retain pointwise/sham controls. No predicted public0.7000 claim follows from an exposed-cohort pilot.

Implementation nuance verified against the [paper HTML appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05688v1) and [JAX GELU documentation](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/_autosummary/jax.nn.gelu.html): the paper code calls JAX GELU with its default approximate=True. This pilot deliberately uses PyTorch exact GELU. The sin/sinc formulas are paper-inspired but are not a bit-exact reproduction of its JAX implementation. Exact-versus-tanh-approximate GELU is a separate controlled follow-up; do not relabel the completed pilot.
