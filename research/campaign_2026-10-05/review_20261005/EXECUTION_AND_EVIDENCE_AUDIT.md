# Execution and evidence audit — 5 October 2026

## Readout

The strongest measured public predictor remains the complete seed-4 package at **0.6660**; the seed-3 package scored **0.6655**. The expected public milestone of 0.7000 remains unmet by evidence. None of the E470–E489 pilot deltas can be added to 0.6660 or treated as a forecast. The public submissions, local paired screens, held-out-within-validation diagnostics, and small-model pilot panels answer different questions.

The most consequential gap is transfer. The activation accounting says **224/236 fit slots** completed, but most were one-epoch H64 diagnostics, with roughly 61,058 parameters in the native two-layer baseline, trained on 24 fit components and compared on the same 16 historically exposed evaluation components. The separate sequence-component screen used smaller H32 models, including an 8,235-parameter GRU control. The current complete predictor is a substantially different GRU256 plus teacher/EMA and fixed final-head procedure. E488 is the useful exception on duration (12 continuous fits, three epochs), but it is still H64, still on the same exposed 16 components, and has no full-predictor or public confirmation. Fit slots are not 224 independent hypotheses, replications, architectures, or predictor-level comparisons.

This is not a reason to discard the pilot work. It has narrowed plausible mechanisms, exposed optimizer/placement/schedule interactions, found implementation failures before wider spend, and produced reusable controls. It is a reason to make the next compute answer a complete-predictor transfer question, rather than spend another window ranking tiny variants on the same components.

## Evidence hierarchy and transfer limits

### Public evidence

The public result is the only direct evidence here about the competition’s public evaluator. E450 records the seed-4 ZIP, submission `2e5j6bnt`, at 0.6660 and seed-3 at 0.6655. The local seed4-minus-seed3 paired difference is +0.001872 with a 95% component interval [-0.000975, +0.006931]; the public increment is +0.0005. The interval is conditional on already-fitted models and cannot calibrate a narrow local-to-public mapping. The public change confirms that the complete recipe transfers at least once; it does not attribute the gain to parent, head, optimizer, chunk size, or bootstrap individually. See [E450](../../campaign_2026-10-04/E450_SEED4_PAIRED_REPORT.md), [public reflection](../../campaign_2026-10-04/PUBLIC_RESULTS_REFLECTION_20261004_LATE.md), and [the research wrap](../RESEARCH_WRAP_2026-10-05.md).

E450's fixed validation panels were historically exposed. Its paired component bootstrap does not account for selecting the seed or recipe after prior panel inspection. Its 0.700–0.702 fixed-validation values are not public-score estimates. The later strong-predictor diagnostic is even more explicit: its original 16-component “fitted” panel was fitted by both predictors, and its additional 16 “excluded” components have no direct row overlap but remain graph-linked to fitted rows (16/16 parent components and 15/16 head components). It reports .774416 on fitted rows and .742757 on the linked excluded panel for the complete predictor, but neither is an independent holdout. Do not juxtapose these values as if they measured public generalization. See [strong-predictor diagnostics](../target_mechanics/strong_baseline/REPORT.md).

### Repeated pilot evaluation

The campaign retrospective identifies 24 fit-role components and 16 historically exposed test components for most activation work. Bootstrap intervals resample components for a fixed fitted model; they do not include seed variation, search/selection, or multiplicity. Reporting every registered arm and contrast is good practice, but it does not undo repeated panel exposure. Several experiments add seeds, which tests fixed-panel seed sensitivity; it does not create fresh evaluation components. In particular, the three-seed E470/E472/E475/E478/E479/E480/E481/E483 families must remain seed-separated. Their component intervals are not population intervals over seeds.

The hard event-output hold is a strong negative for that specific deployment rule: complete WP .774416 versus .692929 on the fitted panel and .742757 versus .666532 on the linked row-excluded panel; class-3 WP also collapses. But this does **not** reject a learned causal transition/posterior update that continues updating during quiet runs. Likewise the strong-model residual diagnostic finds 84–85% difference-mode weighted error energy, but target variance is itself mostly in that mode, and hard sign×magnitude independence already lost a matched cheap control. These facts motivate joint conditional distribution and residual-head tests; they do not establish the mechanism or justify a post-hoc correction.

E468’s five fixed joint-target heads all fell below their historical GRU128 parent in the one-pass frozen-feature comparison; the best candidate delta was about -0.00055. That is useful evidence against those exact coarse-codebook/frozen-trunk/readout implementations. It does not reject a jointly trained representation, a finer or train-only learned support, a different proper likelihood, or a causal posterior memory. In particular, the E468 negative is not a reason to ignore E421’s related branch-head result; E421/E468 differ in fitting procedure and need a direct full-predictor control.

### Genuine pilot patterns, with their limits

- Activation shape is not a single mechanism. E470/E472 found sine-versus-GELU gains on the fixed panel with optimizer dependence; E473’s GELU-plus-linear control challenged a specifically periodic explanation; E474 found embedding effects changed sign across seeds while head-only effects were near zero; E481’s matched-center cubic comparison further weakens the claim that sine curvature itself explains the gains. Keep linear/slope/cubic controls alive.
- Recurrent bounded candidate effects are promising but not settled. E478’s H64 alpha=.1 estimates were positive at all three seeds (+.00270, +.01067, +.00448), with two conditional component intervals excluding zero. E479’s learned-alpha effects were smaller and seed/optimizer dependent. These remain same-panel pilot results, not evidence of a 0.7 public predictor.
- E476’s large single-seed warm-cosine effects did not replicate. In E480’s two new seeds, all eight primary warm-cosine-minus-constant-mean point estimates were negative and six intervals were below zero. “Equal mean LR” is not optimizer-update equivalence, but this sign reversal rejects a seed-robust benefit claim from E476 alone.
- E488 is the most directly useful multi-epoch head result: its three seeds show E3 fixed-mixture-minus-GELU +.00893, +.00431, +.00869 (two component intervals exclude zero, one crosses); recurrent-minus-static is near zero at E3 with all three intervals crossing zero. The E3−E1 duration interaction is positive in all seeds, only one interval excludes zero. This favors testing the fixed mixture as a candidate readout transfer; it does not show recurrent context adds information, and it is not yet a full-incumbent result. Its 12 fits are continuous three-epoch trajectories with 24 saved snapshots, not 24 independent fits.
- E483 sine-minus-baseline is positive across all nine seed-by-Muon-momentum contrasts, yet momentum rankings and shape-by-momentum interactions vary by seed. This is a fixed-panel finding within tested recipes, not a universal momentum setting.
- E486's frozen-weight counterfactual reports positive training-by-inference interactions, but the diagonal cells repeat E483 and changing recurrence at inference changes hidden states, output scale, and clipping. It cannot isolate optimizer causality or prove a representation mechanism.
- E482’s six remote T4 fits repeat E481’s same initialization, so they are hardware/numerical reproducibility evidence rather than an independent seed replication. Its six fits were completed and independently graded, with raw contrasts reproduced. The same-seed cubic-minus-sine Muon contrast is numerically sensitive across hardware; do not count the second hardware run as new market evidence.
- E487 concerns a one-batch optimizer arithmetic replay, not learning: FP32 orthogonalization passed the strict saved-gradient update comparison while BF16 failed. Saved-array arithmetic was independently recomputed; the replay’s raw gradient/state evidence is hash-based rather than export of all tensors. This does not establish optimizer convergence or prediction quality.

## Execution and budget accounting

Three notions of “spent” must stay separate:

1. **Actual local observed GPU time.** The retained local runner receipts for E470–E489 include about 6,348 seconds (1.76 local GPU-wall hours) across successful work and failed probes. That sum is not total campaign GPU usage: it overlaps older ledger windows and does not reconcile all historical attempts. E419 separately records 3.074 device-GPU-hours for its QAT jobs/inference/scoring. A current cumulative 80-device-GPU-hour remainder cannot be inferred by subtracting these partial sums.
2. **Actual remote work / provider quota.** E482’s six T4 fits account for about 1,431 fit seconds (0.397 device-hours summed across workers), excluding probes and setup; concurrency means wall time differs. Kaggle quota is a provider-account balance, not campaign device-hours. The Oct. 5 monitor snapshot reported about 19.84 GPUh and 19.96 TPUh remaining, refresh Oct. 10. A reserved timeout or nominal two-GPU allocation is not measured use.
3. **Reserved or planned budgets.** The 80 device-GPUh cap, 24-hour local block, attempt timeouts, E485/E489 fit reservations, and provider quota are separate constraints. E485’s six fits were never admitted after the strict native-step comparison failed. E489’s six-fit reservation never became training: the original probe failed CLI import before CUDA/data, and the admission arrived at 07:00:32.527, 32.527 seconds after the registered cutoff. A repaired `--help` import check is not a GPU probe. The late attempt consumes no scientific fit slot, but any measured probe time from the late call should still be charged if the provider actually ran it.

The E488 original 900-second resource gate failed honestly: after its actual probe, the measured projection was 1,075.263 seconds. Report both facts. Do not say the original 900-second budget passed, and do not describe the amendment as a scientific change.

W384 v7 is an operationally censored attempt, not a negative width result. Its two one-batch T4 probes passed and both four-epoch workers started, but the W256 process tree reached 8,597,594,112 bytes RSS against the 8 GiB limit; the paired phase stopped, with W384 at 8,470,228,992 bytes. Both were only at epoch 0, batch 120/373, with no validation caches or complete checkpoints. Host available memory fell to about 846.6 MB. The report cannot apportion the RSS rise among Arrow decoding, queued/pinned batches, allocator retention, and file cache. A pre-fit cache-drop hint did not prevent rereads. Do not treat W384 as scientifically worse; fix/measure the memory path before another admitted fit. ## What the evidence does **not** justify rejecting

- E468 does not rule out a properly trained joint distribution/residual head; its exact one-pass, coarse-support, frozen-trunk implementation lost to its parent.
- The strong-model hard event hold does not rule out a learned causal state update that updates during quiet periods. A freeze is a different intervention.
- The small-panel weak attention result, a negative expert architecture contrast, or spline/SSM results do not rule out the same ideas at matched capacity and with the complete incumbent training procedure.
- E485’s strict native-step miss rules out admission of that exact custom H128 optimizer implementation under the registered numerical gate; it does not prove all FP32 custom recurrences fail. E487’s later successful FP32 arithmetic replay also does not retroactively admit E485.
- E462’s linker incident and E419’s post-fit copy error say nothing about predictive value. They do identify packaging/provenance requirements.
- Lack of a public score for repaired q8 packages is neither a score failure nor a deployment pass.

## Prioritized next full-predictor comparisons

The next window should select a very small number of candidate mechanisms and run them through the **complete GRU256 parent/EMA/final-head procedure**, with candidate and control trained identically and all arms retained. Freeze the held-out component plan before any prediction-array reads; use at least two fresh training seeds, and keep per-seed results rather than pool seeds into a pseudo-replicate. Component-bootstrap within a seed is useful for panel uncertainty but is not a substitute for seed replication or a new evaluation cohort.

1. **Transfer E488’s fixed readout mixture first.** Compare the incumbent GELU head with the fixed 50/50 GELU/LeakyReLU mixture under the same parent checkpoint, teacher/EMA schedule, head fine-tune, objective, initialization stream, and inference implementation. Include a parameter-identical GELU control and a frozen-weight head-swap arm so the test distinguishes activation replacement from additional fitting. E488 supplies the strongest multi-seed, multi-epoch small-model signal; recurrent context is near-zero versus static at epoch 3, so do not spend first on a dynamic gate.
2. **Transfer one recurrent candidate with shape controls.** Compare native GELU against the bounded candidate and a center/slope-matched cubic or linear control on the same incumbent-sized trunk, optimizer, initialization, and full schedule. Predeclare one placement and retain the arm if it loses. The H64 E478 signal merits this test, while E473/E481 warn that “sine” may be a proxy for local slope/shape and effects interact with optimizer. This is a distinct question from the E488 readout mixture; do not combine both changes in the first full-predictor comparison.
3. **If the joint-target path remains the priority, test one matched residual/distribution head end-to-end.** Keep the current parent output as an exact residual anchor; fit a train-only support/codebook and shared joint distribution with target-specific weighted Bayes readouts; compare to equal-capacity regression and shuffled-support controls. Use the same complete training budget and event/quiet inputs. Report the official clipped weighted-Pearson score and class/target slices; do not optimize support or thresholds on the repeatedly exposed evaluation panel. Preserve causal updates during quiet runs. This directly addresses the E468 limitations and the strong baseline’s difference-mode residual diagnostic without assuming their mechanism is correct.

For each comparison, report: full predictor score under the official procedure; same-seed paired component scores; class and target breakdowns; raw finite checks before clipping; native inference latency/reset/causality/ABI checks; and actual resource receipts. Package and submit only after the actual deployment checks pass; public improvement remains unconfirmed until a new public score measures transfer. Do not use the best conditional pilot point estimate as a candidate expected gain.

## Deployability gates versus scientific gates

Keep the two kinds of gate distinct.

**Deployability guard:** the exact candidate artifact must load in the scorer’s runtime/ABI, preserve streaming reset and causal semantics, match the Python reference within predeclared inference tolerances, remain finite, satisfy memory and runtime limits, and pass the complete submission callback. E462 demonstrates why the actual Python 3.11/linker environment matters. This is a guard on whether a predictor can be submitted; it is not a demand that a changed scientific method reproduce the incumbent’s training trajectory.

**Scientific/implementation gate:** parity checks, full-shape CUDA probes, and strict update tolerances are appropriate when the registered experiment claims a numerically equivalent optimizer/recurrence path or when exact equivalence is needed to isolate one changed factor. Keep those numerical tolerances fixed once observed; do not widen a failed gate to admit a candidate. Conversely, do not invent a universal “training-equivalence” requirement for a new model whose intended treatment is a different architecture, optimizer, or training procedure. For those experiments, hold all *unchanged* factors constant, document the changed training recipe, compare complete predictors, and apply the independent deployability guard separately.

For the next proposed full-predictor arms, predeclare whether a parity check is an implementation-equivalence gate or only a reference diagnostic. Preserve any already registered numerical limits (including E485’s strict gate and E487’s recorded FP32/BF16 comparison) without retroactively changing them. No numerical failure should be turned into a predictive conclusion; no predictive gain should waive runtime/ABI failure.
