AlexKlos — 09.03.2026, 10:24
I do not have extensive experience in machine learning, and this was my first participation in a competition of this kind.

I describe only the approaches that actually worked for me in the WunderChallenge.

The base model used in the solution was a Transformer.

Difficulty-stratified split.
In the solution I used a difficulty-stratified split, where the dataset is split according to the complexity of individual time series sequences.

To estimate the sequence difficulty, a baseline Transformer model (after HPO) was used. The model predicts the next step of the time series based on the previous observation window.

For each sequence the following metric is computed:

difficulty(seq) = mean(MSE_prediction)

All sequences are then sorted by difficulty. Extreme values are removed using quantile clipping, and the validation subset is selected from sequences that uniformly cover the remaining difficulty range.

This approach did not provide a direct improvement in the evaluation metric, but it significantly stabilized the training process, improved the comparability of experimental results, and enabled the method described in section 6 below.

PCA Clustering
When projecting the original features (raw32) into a 3-dimensional PCA space, two distinct clusters become clearly visible.

I used the distance from each point (the input raw32 vector) to the plane separating these clusters (the plane is shown in red in the figure).

The resulting value was used as an additional feature.

This approach provided a small improvement in the evaluation metric.

GRU-Based Online Feature Extraction
A lightweight GRU model was used as an online feature extractor.

The GRU was trained to predict the next step of the sequence on the entire dataset. After training, the hidden state of the last GRU layer was used as an additional signal and fed into the Transformer model.

This approach produced a very significant improvement in the evaluation metric.

Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)
Simple averaging of model weights from checkpoints obtained during the last epochs of training (using different combinations) produced a strong improvement in the evaluation metric.

Unfortunately, I did not have enough time to fully implement this approach before the submission deadline, so it was not included in the final solution.

Training on the Entire Dataset
Another effective technique was training on the entire dataset.

If a difficulty-stratified split is used (see above) and experiments are conducted with different train/validation ratios, the target metric (R²) shows a stable increase when the training set size grows. This suggests that the model benefits from additional training data.

In this setup the leaderboard is used as the final evaluation metric, while the validation set is used only to detect the onset of overfitting by monitoring the divergence between train and validation metrics.

After identifying the optimal hyperparameters and the epoch corresponding to the best validation performance, the model is retrained on the entire dataset using these parameters.

This approach provided a strong improvement in the final score.

P.S. 
Using the complexity of each raw32 input vector at every time step of the sequence as an additional feature (similar to the approach described in section 2) did not produce positive results, although I spent considerable time testing multiple implementations of such a feature.

This may indicate either that I was unable to find the correct relationship between these parameters, or that such a relationship does not exist.

Intuitively, one might expect the complexity of each time step in a market state time series to depend on noise or volatility, which in turn should be related to the dynamics of market parameters (price, volatility, spread, etc.).

However, the experimental results suggest that the dataset might consist of synthetic data that imitates market behavior, with noise added independently from the underlying signal.

If this assumption is correct, the quality of the synthetic data could potentially be improved by introducing a dependency between the noise component and the underlying data.
