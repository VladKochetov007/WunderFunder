# Task: generate new, mechanism-distinct ideas for an anonymous sequence-prediction problem

You are an external ideator. You have read-only access to this text only. Produce 8 ideas that are *different in mechanism* from each other and from what is already tried (list below). For each idea give: (1) name, (2) the mechanism in 2-4 sentences, (3) why it could help given the measurements below, (4) the cheapest experiment (hours, one GPU), (5) what result would falsify it, (6) expected size of the gain in the competition metric (be honest; say "unknown" if you cannot guess), (7) mechanism family label. Include at least two unconventional ideas (from physics, signal processing, information theory, control, neuroscience, quant finance microstructure, compression, game theory, program synthesis) and at least one idea about *measurement or data structure* rather than a model. Do not propose anything that needs labels from the future, other competitors' weights, or external data. Do not pad: fewer, sharper ideas beat a long list.

## The problem
- Data: sequences of 20,000 rows, 32 anonymous numeric features per row (anonymised order-book-like event data), strictly causal prediction of two targets (t0, t1) per row; rows are scored only after the first 99. About 12,000 training sequences plus 1,873 validation sequences. Sequences are linked into chains (component-disjoint folds are used for validation). Inference must be causal, deterministic, on 1 CPU core within 60 minutes for the whole hidden test set (about 39M rows); this limits the *served* model to roughly one 2-layer GRU of hidden width 256 (74 microseconds per row); training can be arbitrarily large (teachers, ensembles, distillation).
- Metric: weighted Pearson correlation, pooled over all scored rows and both targets, with weights |clip(y, -2, 2)| and predictions/labels clipped at 2. Public leaderboard score of our best package: 0.6664. A teammate team is at a similar level. Goal: > 0.700.
- Labels are piecewise constant in time: mean run length of a constant label is about 9.5 rows. Sequences fall into four "market classes" by label statistics; class 3 carries roughly half the metric weight and has by far the lowest correlation (0.44 versus 0.74-0.81 for classes 0-2). Hidden-test loss relative to validation is concentrated in class 3.

## Measured facts (validation fold 1, 468 sequences; fold-to-fold noise: single-run standard deviation of a comparison is about 0.0022)
- Error decomposition of our best student (WP 0.6694): replacing the model's *magnitude* by the true magnitude (keeping the model's sign) lifts WP to only 0.685; replacing the model's *sign* by the true sign (keeping the model's magnitude) lifts WP to 0.843 (class 3: 0.44 -> 0.77). Weighted sign accuracy is 84.3% overall and 80.7% in class 3. So almost all the loss is in getting the *direction* right, not the size.
- Sign accuracy versus the age of the current constant-label run: 90.7% for age 0 (the row where the label has just changed), 90.5% age 1, 90.9% ages 2-3, 87.8% ages 4-7, 85.2% ages 8-15, 84.0% ages 16-31, 82.1% ages 32-63, 81.1% ages 64-127, 77.3% ages 128-511, 74.0% beyond 512. Error mass is spread: 19.9% of all weighted sign errors sit in runs older than 128 rows. (Surprising: the model is *most* accurate right after a label change.)
- Hyper-parameters are on a plateau: width 384 vs 256 gives +0.0013 on average over three folds; 3 epochs equals 4 epochs; Muon optimiser vs AdamW is +0.004; schedules, dropout, depth, losses (Huber and correlation losses are harmful), class weights and EMA are all within noise. Averaging seeds adds +0.0015-0.002 but cannot be served. Distilling a wider teacher into the served-width student does not transfer. Four teacher families correlate 0.95-0.99, so re-mixing their targets cannot change the distillation target by more than a few percent of its variance.
- Class 3 is data-limited: halving data costs class 3 0.009; a class-3-only specialist is far worse (-0.14); synthetic labels hurt.
- Already tried (do not re-propose): GRU/LSTM/TCN/gated/two-clock variants, Muon multiplier, EMA/SWA, bootstrap targets (training-time substitution of the teacher prediction h rows ahead), family-aux heads, next-change auxiliary heads, label-weight power, correlation/Huber/mix losses, wider models up to 768, chrono/orthogonal initialisations, schedules (one-cycle, WSD, SGDR), propensity weighting, seed ensembles, mirror-symmetry by block swap (failed), class-3 specialists, pseudo-label noisy student, valid bagging, rank transforms, Transformers of small size (null), blending the organiser baseline (null for distilled models).
- Not yet tried: selective state-space (SSM) teachers, exact bid/ask mirror decoding from the event code tables, mixture/quantile output heads, sign-classification heads, explicit change-point / regime-switching state, test-time (causal) adaptation of the state or of a bias term, learned feature-specific time constants, hierarchical (multi-rate) recurrent stacks, predictive-coding style losses.
