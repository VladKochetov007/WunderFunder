# Descriptive external publication comparison

The measured external prefixes do not identify the competition source or generator. They do show that publication policy can create the synchronous price/quantity/trade refresh patterns seen in competition code fields, while nonlinear feature coding can preserve refresh equality and substantially distort quantity ratios. No external numeric state or transformation enters the competition pipeline.

`preregistration.json` fixed 32 existing TRAIN groups, both instruments, and external prefixes before reading these feature blocks. The competition pass reads all 104 instrument fields, with no new targets, sequence IDs, masks or additional `a` fields. External inputs are exactly 20,000 original UTF-8 messages each from BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT and USDCUSDT and 1,200 MD snapshots merged with 13,487 strictly earlier receive-time trades. Consumed-prefix hashes are in `results.json`; these are not full-file hashes. There were zero MD book/trade timestamp ties. No symbol/time alignment was attempted. The prior external depth study validated reconstruction; this pass uses observed physical top quotes only and makes no new depth-reconstruction claim.

Each source is split into chronological first two thirds and last third. Hold, transient-trade-clear, coalesce-five-message, and delay-trade-five-message policies are explicit alternatives, separately reinitialized within each partition. Initialization is unknown: emitted external rows before the first positive bid/ask are censored. The two earlier uncensored script/results versions are preserved, because the added jump descriptor exposed artificial zero-to-first-quote jumps. `initialization_correction.json` records this fix. Competition code fields remain uncensored, because their sign/zero has unidentified physical meaning.

Competition i0 has price-code event rates 20.93%/19.27%, quantity-code rates 90.66%/90.35%, and trade-block refresh rates 32.04%/30.78% in the two partitions, pooled over 32 groups. Its price changes co-refresh quantity on 99.97% of events and trade fields on 96.31%/96.28%. Competition i1 has price-code event rates 9.31%/11.02%, quantity rates 43.87%/46.92%, trade refresh rates 26.72%/29.56%, and trade co-refresh on 93.63%/94.64% of price events. Cross-instrument publication is therefore visibly asymmetric on this TRAIN sample.

Held external top quotes have zero simultaneous price/trade refresh by construction: one raw message updates a quote or a trade. Transient clearing permits a book message immediately after a trade to change both blocks; message coalescing and trade delay can also create synchronous updates. BTC/ETH transient, coalesce5 and delay5 alternatives yield price/trade co-refresh fractions around 13–60%, below the competition's 94–96%. MD transient reaches about 92–96%, coalesce5 reaches 100%, and delay5 about 86–92%. MD's joint event rates still differ strongly: held MD trade refresh is about 88–89% and held quantity refresh about 7–10%, versus competition i0's 31–32% and 90–91%. No single tested policy is thereby selected, and matching one conditional statistic is insufficient.

The implication is a serializer/refresh hypothesis, not synchronous exchange causation. Competition field co-change may reflect combined batches, trade-field clearing, an encoding response to book updates, or a transformed state updated together. It does not establish that book and trades arrived simultaneously or that either caused the other. The `dp`/`dv` blocks are only named trade-like fields here; their physical semantics are not independently identified. A co-change representation is a legitimate current/past feature, but importing external coordinates or policy parameters would not be justified.

Publication-row gap tails are highly source-dependent. Median within-group competition price-event gaps are 1 row for both instruments in both partitions, although long plateaus coexist with clusters of consecutive code events. Held BTC/ETH medians are 2–3 message rows; held MD medians are 13/9 rows. USDC has no changed top price in either prefix partition. These are gaps under unequal publication/sampling schemes. They are not physical event arrival rates, elapsed-time comparisons, or matches of market regimes. Competition uses 22-field price code equality, while these external measurements use two physical top prices, and that representation difference alone may alter event counts.

An adaptive registered addendum measures each price-block L1 change divided by its partition's median nonzero L1 change. Competition's median across groups of within-group q90/q99 is about 1.43/1.63–1.65 for i0 and 1.55/1.80–1.86 for i1. Held physical BTC q90/q99 is about 6.02/21.86 and 8.22/37.40; ETH about 6.50/21.64 and 4.00/11.00; MD about 2.55/5.00 and 2.40/5.00. These descriptors remove a common linear price unit only. Unknown per-column nonlinear coding, code support, 22-versus-2 block width, and unequal sampling make physical jump-law comparisons unidentified. Narrow encoded tails do not imply narrow physical price jumps.

Positive-quantity change absolute log-ratios have competition median-across-group q90/q99 around .35–.38/1.80–1.96 for i0 and .58–.60/2.27–2.37 for i1. External held values range from near zero for USDC to about 3.05–3.40/6.08–6.29 for MD. Only 49% of i0 and 53% of i1 encoded quantity values are positive; raw physical quotes here are positive by definition. Competition encoded ratios therefore are neither complete nor identified physical quantity ratios. No quantity-unit equivalence is claimed.

The deterministic transform falsifier applies `log1p` directly to measured positive physical bid/ask quantities. All measured equality-change flags are preserved, while more than 98% of BTC and more than 99% of ETH/USDC/MD quantity ratios change by over 1%. This uses a declared example transform, not an estimated competition inverse. It establishes the useful boundary: equality/co-change survives an injective coordinate transformation, while volume ratios, jump magnitudes and physical geometry generally do not. Non-injective clipping would also lose equality and requires a separate falsifier. The exact ratio error quantiles and sample counts are preserved.
