# Pre-score rule for future forecast models

The scientific transfer prior is currently null, deliberately: the existing
public data do not identify a gain-transfer distribution for the clean panel's
new families. The frozen sensitivity grid is not a posterior distribution.

A later numeric transport model must be committed before its author/auditor sees
the quality of the candidate to which that model will be applied. Its parameters
may be learned from separately identified historical bridge data, with source,
selection and ancestry checks. Its priors may reflect independently supported
mechanisms and fitting-domain evidence, with explicit sensitivity. They must not
be chosen after observing the candidate's improvement or the coefficient needed
to reach 0.6660.

Candidate quality can update a local-effect posterior inside a model committed
in advance. It cannot justify choosing a favorable transfer coefficient by
itself. If newly acquired bridge evidence requires changing assumptions after
candidate quality is exposed, preserve the original forecast, label the revision
as post-score and require independent criticism. That revision must not be
presented as the original pre-score forecast or hide selection risk.

An auditor may explain qualitatively why a large stable effect is promising
under scientific transport assumptions. A forecast mean crossing the target
still needs a numeric model with an honest provenance and independent audit.
