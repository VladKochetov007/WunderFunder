2. **Why it should work.** Fixed TBPTT uses the same detach horizon even when the gradient tail changes during optimization or across training regimes. The proposed controller measures φ_k(s)=||∂L_s/∂h_{s-k}|| on 32 reproducibly sampled training-only loss positions, with maximum lag R=2000 rows once at the start of each epoch. For the 32 probe positions S, let φ̄_k=|S|⁻¹Σ_s∈Sφ_k(s); estimate log β as the maximum pairwise slope (log φ̄_k′−log φ̄_k)/(k′−k) for 1800≤k<k′≤2000. Use the Aicher-style empirical relative-tail proxy (paper equations 23–25 and the authors’ empirical denominator simplification) to choose the smallest K in {500,1000,2000} whose estimated relative bias is at most δ=0.10. If estimated decay is nonfinite, not decaying, or no candidate meets δ, use K=2000 and log the invalid or unmet budget. These probe size, cadence, lag range, δ, and fallback are proposed design choices, not measured or universally safe values. The estimate is not asserted to equal true error or certify a bound. Before any fit, compare the proxy with exact full-versus-truncated gradient error on controlled synthetic recurrences with geometric, delayed, sparse-spike, and nonmonotone tails; flag every underestimation and do not proceed with this controller if the gate exposes one. A likely alternative explanation for any endpoint change is altered optimization cadence or noise; this comparison changes detach horizon only, keeps optimizer timing fixed, and records chosen K and probe cost.

3. **Compute advantage.** Shorter K may reduce retained backward graph and activation memory while leaving all causal forward rows and inference state unchanged. Probe cost is part of the treatment and may erase savings. The proposed useful cost result is at least 15% lower end-to-end training time, including probes, at the noninferiority and robustness gate in section 8; no such saving is measured. There is no expected serving speedup: the inference recurrence and model are unchanged, so the adaptive controller adds no serving work. Existing CPU-serving performance remains unmeasured for this proposed endpoint. If the method is later used for a teacher, its usefulness must be graded through the same compact student route in a separately declared comparison.

The adaptive arm selects K from {500,1000,2000} using the training-only probe above, once per epoch. Both stream 500-row microchunks and perform one optimizer update per 2000 input rows. K controls graph horizon only: it must not alter the 2000-row optimizer clock, effective loss normalization, optimizer state, LR schedule, or update count. In particular, the authors’ BPTT(2K,K) update cadence and associated square-root-K learning-rate scaling are not imported; they define a different trainer. 8. **Decision-changing result.** These are proposed decision thresholds, not measurements. A useful outcome must show at least 15% end-to-end time reduction including probes, using the equal-fold mean of paired reductions and reporting timing uncertainty. It must also be noninferior in official WP: the lower 97.5% family-adjusted seed and component intervals for Δ must be greater than −0.0005, using the declared seed/component uncertainty protocol. Systematic or concentrated fold regression prevents automatic promotion and requires a bounded follow-up. A synthetic underestimation, changed optimizer cadence, failed state replay, or missing B300 profile blocks admission rather than being interpreted as a score result. No improvement in total time, failure of WP noninferiority, or repeated fallback to K=2000 makes this controller variant unhelpful at the tested settings; it does not refute all adaptive credit methods. A zero-crossing interval alone is not a universal kill rule.
