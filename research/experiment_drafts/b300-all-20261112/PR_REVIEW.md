## Shared L4 scientific and online contract

The shared contract below is the one used by the recent L4 PR77–80 packets; earlier B300 and L4 experiments have their own frozen recipes.

### Data, roster, and masks

- Official fit roster: 10,325 whole train sequences, zero validation fitting. Official validation: 1,873 sequences and 3,528,273 scored rows. Split SHA-256: 0c4de3a253d23b3a5d5ca73b7aeb7fcae2b974673e4f9ec51e94e593c59d114c. PR80’s latest comment reports common cell data hash 4987ed5f263cf3c655714a7af3e792605716f6cb256c1c96474eb46ae9869323. Exact sparse membership is bound by the source/data receipts; the comments expose counts and hashes, not the complete ID list.
- A row group is one 20,000-row sequence with 112 raw inputs and t0/t1 targets. The input columns comprise two instruments’ price/volume/derivative groups plus a0–a7. seq_ix is a boundary identifier, not chronological order. See the [pinned data reader](https://github.com/VladKochetov007/WunderFunder/blob/7e4074314a62d0907c9608f9220cf72958708a2d/src/wnn/data.py).
- Training uses all_after_warmup; train has no is_scored source column. Fit normalization uses train-only all_fit statistics. Validation selection is need_prediction AND is_scored.
- The [PR68 contract](https://github.com/VladKochetov007/WunderFunder/blob/13350588eafa8cd38d3df76fb4a9a38c1ccf81f6/research/experiment_drafts/architecture-execution-20261107/CONTRACT.md) also records the 99-row warm-up and explicitly distinguishes TBPTT chunk length from sequence length.

### Online state and metric

- The scorer presents one row at a time. It calls the model on every row so state advances during warm-up; for rows 0–98 it expects no prediction, then two finite predictions on each requested row. is_scored is evaluator-only, not a predictor input. See the pinned [callback/evaluator](https://github.com/VladKochetov007/WunderFunder/blob/7e4074314a62d0907c9608f9220cf72958708a2d/utils.py).
- Causal state resets at sequence boundaries and carries across TBPTT chunks; chunk boundaries truncate gradients, not history. PR79 uses chunk1000 and carries K16’s preceding 15 normalized rows across chunks. PR80 K64 extends this stem history to 63 earlier rows. The recent packets prohibit future inputs and chronological ordering inferred from seq_ix.
- [global_wp_clip2_v1](https://github.com/VladKochetov007/WunderFunder/blob/7e4074314a62d0907c9608f9220cf72958708a2d/src/wnn/gmetric.py) clips target and prediction to ±2, weights each row by the absolute clipped target, pools all selected rows separately per target, then averages t0 and t1. It is not a mean of sequence-level correlations.

## Duplicate and overlap flags for portfolio design

- PR80 K64 is a longer-history extension of PR79 K16; PR74 tested causal input stems on a Transformer. These are related architecture-specific results, not three independent mechanism families.
- PR80 width512 revisits capacity scaling after PR61’s wider-GRU results were below its GRU256x2 control. It is a new recipe (GRU512x2 with K16), but width is not an untouched direction.
- PR80 dropout0.1 overlaps PR71 dropout0.2 and PR68’s reported late-epoch overfit. The new setting is a specific interaction with the K16 stem.
- Other covered families: target specialization (77), train-only input-dynamics pretraining (78), dropout/SWA/event211 (71), TL3/Transformer fixed-mixture objectives (67/69/70), clipping/robust losses (62/63), optimizer/TBPTT horizon (72), alternative architectures (68), Transformer recency/gating/SwiGLU/QK normalization (64/65/66/75), and TL3 role/residual analysis (73).
- All validation results are development-exposed. A new PR, seed, architecture wrapper, or model filename does not create an independent holdout; any follow-up needs a predeclared contrast, exact lineage, fixed endpoint, complete paired results, and its own receiver receipts.
