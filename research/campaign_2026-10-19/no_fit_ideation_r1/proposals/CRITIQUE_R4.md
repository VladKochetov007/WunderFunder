# Critique R4 — state geometry R2 and root questions

Short attack on the frozen proposals. Unknown task semantics remain testable assumptions, not stop gates.

## State geometry R2

1. **Shared sparse measurement operator.** The treatment is only distinct from an ordinary shared linear encoder if sparsity/low rank and identifiability are actually enforced. With an unconstrained residual escape, `Dz+r` can be a reparameterized identity map; rotations/scales also leave `D,z` non-identifiable. Symbolic recovery checks the solver, not whether real channels share a basis. Specify the constraint and compare against a matched shared dense encoder and identity residual. Precomputing an inverse may be cheap at inference, but fitting/conditioning the dictionary is the main risk.

2. **Input-only conditional graph.** This is a plausible new predictive sparsity treatment, distinct from code-transition topology, but “edge” means conditional predictability, not physical causation. Common-factor estimation, feature selection across families, and multiple testing can create unstable masks. Sequence-partition stability plus degree-matched shuffled masks is a useful screen; the actual falsifier is a matched masked-versus-dense model. Keep all edge selection training-only. Cost is moderate and should include graph-screening overhead.

3. **Jordan-split innovations.** Separate positive and negative accumulators are a real operator difference from generic event clocks: they retain reversal order and total variation. Toy paths prove that representational property only. Assumption: directional excursions have different persistence than a signed net change. Compare against one signed accumulator and an equal-state-size recurrent baseline; reject if the split is not incrementally useful. Low-to-moderate state cost.

4. **Orientation × amplitude head.** The claimed factorization does not generally recover the metric-tilted conditional mean. Separate sign loss and absolute-amplitude loss estimate separate conditional quantities; their product is not generally `E[|Y|Y|X]/E[|Y||X]`, which is the weighted-MSE/WP population target. A toy distribution can expose this counterexample, so the stated toy test must compare the product against that exact target, not merely show flexible asymmetry. Use a joint weighted objective on `q·m` or predict sufficient signed moments with a derived readout; then compare with the direct scalar head. Unknown target semantics are irrelevant to this algebraic flaw.

5. **Prior prediction plus current assimilation.** A direct current-input route is already present in the GRU model’s `z` skip, and Belief already combines a dense recurrent state with current evidence. So “the model lacks a fast path” is false. The new treatment is a prior-state prediction error `E(x_t)-Hh_t^-` with a correction operator; keep that exact distinction and compare its correction/readout ablations against the existing current encoder skip and ordinary GRU/Belief. A synthetic delayed step proves pathway behavior, not that current models fail to assimilate observations. Cost is moderate.

6. **Frozen warm-up context.** This is distinct from H-RR17-06 normalization: it preserves a static target-free context branch after the 99 warm-up rows. But the existing recurrent model already processes those rows, so paired sequences that differ only in warm-up prove only that the new branch can retain information. Compare ordinary dynamic processing of the same warm-up with static initialization-plus-skip, and against a raw/dynamic control. Assumption: warm-up has useful sequence context that ordinary recurrence fails to retain. Low cost; falsify if the static branch adds no pooled WP or hurts.

7. **Passivity-bounded state.** This constrains hidden-state dynamics; it does not inherit any financial conservation law. The symbolic matrix check only proves a chosen inequality. The “dissipation” slack is not automatically a predictive feature, and a contractive state may suppress legitimate signal amplification. Call it a speculative stability regularizer, not market passivity. Compare same-size constrained and unconstrained models and measure bounded perturbation response as well as WP; cost may be higher than moderate if spectral constraints are enforced each update.

## Root notes

- **Weighted-MSE proof:** The population argument is sound under the score-weighted evaluation distribution. Its boundary matters: it does not establish finite-capacity training behavior, shared-capacity allocation, sample-weight equivalence, or availability of hidden score membership at inference. Keep the proof as theory, not as permission to use `is_scored` online.
- **Target-operator identification:** Distinct from historical model reconstruction and from label-clock timing. Search a declared small family of future-input operators on permitted training components; control selection/multiple-testing and require stable cross-component alignment against simple lag baselines. Failure to find one is inconclusive about all possible target constructions.
- **State aliasing:** A useful diagnostic, not a Bayes ceiling. Matching in 112 dimensions may be sparse and sensitive to scale/common-state controls. Separation by longer histories supports history information; failure to find matches does not prove irreducible noise. Keep support and overlap explicit.
- **Recurrent-state staleness:** Distinct from ARTBP, which changes the gradient estimator. The proposed issue is carrying `h` computed under old parameters into a chunk using new parameters. A fixed-prefix replay comparing stale and re-burned state at actual update cadence is the cheap falsifier; negligible change demotes it.
- **PCGrad / target-gradient conflict:** Negative cosine alone is not evidence of harmful interference; it can occur at a valid joint optimum. First identify persistent conflict tied to target deterioration or magnitude/curvature imbalance. Then compare at matched updates and require improvement in official mean WP plus both target scores; PCGrad can reduce the joint objective.
