# State geometry proposals

These are ten speculative mechanisms for the two-instrument causal sequence task. The data contract supports only stable channel identity and broad feature families; it does not establish depth order, quote semantics for individual indices, target horizon, or a physical scale. All inference-time state must use current and past observations within one sequence, reset at sequence boundaries, and ignore `seq_ix`, targets, and `is_scored`. No fit, data probe, or performance claim was made here. A cited method or finding motivates a transformation only; it is not evidence that it transfers to this dataset.

## 1. Side-specific quote clouds instead of an ordered depth ladder

**Mechanism.** Represent each instrument’s bid-price, ask-price, bid-volume, and ask-volume channels as four side-tagged empirical measures (for example, fixed quantile knots plus mass/dispersion), rather than interpreting the numeric channel index as a spatial/depth coordinate. Keep side identity, but make within-side permutations irrelevant in this branch. The data contract explicitly says the index identifies a recurring component and is not a guaranteed depth rank, so a ladder convolution may impose false locality.

**Assumption / prediction.** The order within each side is partly arbitrary, while the side-level distribution of values carries signal. A set encoding should preserve predictions under a within-side permutation and retain useful level-shape information. **Cheapest test:** no-fit, verify exact permutation invariance on symbolic vectors; then a future matched-capacity fit compares measure encoding with indexed channels. **Falsifier:** identity-bearing indexed channels win consistently. **Alternative:** the stable channel identities are meaningful even without depth ordering. Cost: low-to-moderate. The set-function construction is described in [Deep Sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06114); its applications do not establish market transfer.

## 2. Volume as composition plus total size

**Mechanism.** Encode each instrument’s bid/ask volume blocks as log-ratios of positive components to their geometric mean, plus log total. This separates allocation shape from overall scale without assuming depth order. Zeros require a floor; negative values invalidate this chart.

**Assumption / prediction.** Similar imbalance shapes may carry across liquidity scales; total size remains separate. Prediction: composition contributes when raw magnitudes shift, while shape-only coordinates are invariant to common block rescaling. **Cheapest test:** algebraically verify centered log-ratios are scale-invariant; future fit compares raw, composition-only, and composition-plus-total under matched inputs. **Falsifier:** nonpositive volumes or raw scale dominates. **Alternative:** absolute size is the pressure signal. Cost: low. Aitchison’s simplex/log-ratio treatment motivates the geometry ([1982 paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1982.tb01195.x)); these volumes are not verified compositions.

## 3. Quote shape with an explicit affine gauge

**Mechanism.** Within each instrument and price-like block, compute a robust location and scale from the current row, express channel deviations in that local chart, and pass location and scale as separate coordinates. The model can then learn shape-dependent rules without being forced to rediscover translation and scale normalization from raw values. Preserve channel identity in the normalized residuals; this is not a depth-order assumption.

**Assumption / prediction.** Future-movement indicators depend more on relative quote configuration than on arbitrary sequence-level price origin or units. Prediction: the shape branch should be less sensitive to a fixed affine recoding of a sequence, while the explicit gauge coordinates recover any real level/volatility effect. **Cheapest test:** no-fit, apply a positive affine transform to a symbolic price block and verify the normalized shape is unchanged; future fit tests the split against raw features. **Falsifier:** level/scale coordinates dominate and shape adds nothing. **Alternative:** absolute levels encode regime information. Cost: low. This is speculative because the feature contract does not define the price transform or units.

## 4. Bid/ask reflection as a signed symmetry

**Mechanism.** Build a side-exchange/reflection transform: swap bid and ask blocks, reflect price-like values around a robust within-row center, swap side-specific volumes, and negate the two movement targets during training augmentation. Equivalently, split suitable inputs into components even and odd under this transform and constrain the output to be odd. Use whole side blocks; do not pair channel `k` with a presumed matching depth level.

**Assumption / prediction.** If targets are signed future movements and feature anonymization preserves the economic reflection, mirrored books should imply mirrored outputs. The sharp prediction is an exact odd response under the transform. **Cheapest test:** no-fit algebra check that the transform is an involution and the head is odd; future fit compares the symmetry-constrained model to unconstrained features. **Falsifier:** mirrored examples have systematically non-opposite targets or the constraint costs held-out performance. **Alternative:** learn bid/ask asymmetry softly without imposing parity. Cost: low-to-moderate. This is highly speculative: target semantics are undisclosed, additional channels have no known reflection rule, and the robust center need not be a market midpoint.

## 5. Shared instrument chart, then common and differential coordinates

**Mechanism.** Apply one shared encoder to the corresponding 52-channel schemas for `i0` and `i1`, add a role indicator only after that chart, and form common and differential latent coordinates, `(z0+z1)/2` and `z0-z1`. A role-specific head still predicts `i0`; the construction does not treat the two targets or instruments as interchangeable.

**Assumption / prediction.** Both instruments expose partly shared measurement geometry, while common movement and relative movement play different predictive roles. Prediction: the model should use fewer arbitrary instrument-specific directions and show distinct sensitivity to common-mode versus differential perturbations. **Cheapest test:** a future matched-parameter comparison of shared and unshared charts, with the same recurrent core; no-fit, verify role exchange maps common coordinates to themselves and differential coordinates to their negatives. **Falsifier:** shared encoding suppresses stable `i0`-specific structure. **Alternative:** keep separate encoders and let ordinary fusion discover overlap. Cost: moderate. The schema match is a task fact; shared latent semantics are only an assumption.

## 6. A causal moving numeraire for the instrument pair

**Mechanism.** Reduce each instrument’s price-like block to a robust current location, estimate a bounded exponentially weighted slope `beta` from past changes of `i0` against `i1`, and represent `i0` as residual `x0 - beta*x1` plus the benchmark component and `beta` itself. Update the slope using only rows already observed in the current sequence. This makes the second instrument a changing numeraire rather than simply another concatenated input.

**Assumption / prediction.** `i1` may proxy a shared market component with a locally varying loading. Prediction: residual and common movement will have different target associations, and removing common drift should help when the loading is stable. **Cheapest test:** no-fit toy calculation verifies the causal update uses no future row; future fit compares raw, fixed-beta, and online-beta representations with matched model capacity. **Falsifier:** beta is unstable, near-zero, or residualization removes useful signal. **Alternative:** shared/differential latent fusion can learn nonlinear commonality without a hand-built hedge. Cost: low-to-moderate; needs a safe variance floor and bounded updates. No market relation between these anonymized instruments is established.

## 7. Local covariance as the state’s geometry

**Mechanism.** Maintain a causal exponentially weighted mean and shrinkage covariance for a compact feature block, and encode the current innovation by its whitened direction, Mahalanobis radius, and a compact summary of covariance eigenvalues. The covariance ellipsoid adapts which combinations count as ordinary; preserving the radius and eigenvalue summary prevents whitening from erasing regime intensity. This differs from independent per-channel normalization because it models cross-channel dependence.

**Assumption / prediction.** Predictive meaning depends on deviations from the current joint covariance regime, not just marginal magnitudes. Prediction: equal raw deviations along high- and low-variance correlated directions will be treated differently, with gains concentrated after covariance structure changes. **Cheapest test:** no-fit symbolic affine transformation check for Mahalanobis-radius invariance when mean/covariance transform together; future fit compares diagonal scaling with shrinkage full-covariance geometry. **Falsifier:** cross-covariances are noisy or the diagonal chart matches. **Alternative:** simple robust per-channel scales. Cost: moderate, with numerical regularization and causal updates. The proposed low-rank/compact implementation must avoid using future rows.

## 8. Trade prices located in the quote cloud

**Mechanism.** Treat trade-price channels (`dp`) as points to locate relative to the same instrument’s bid/ask price clouds, rather than as a separate raw feature block. For each trade-price component, form its robust signed distance to each side’s empirical distribution, scaled by quote dispersion, then retain a separate trade-volume weight. The representation asks whether observed trading sits nearer the bid-like or ask-like region without naming any channel as best quote.

**Assumption / prediction.** Trade location relative to available quoted prices contains execution-pressure information that is obscured by separate concatenation. Prediction: an identical trade-price move should have different implications when the quote cloud shifts around it. **Cheapest test:** no-fit, verify invariance to a joint positive affine transform of trade and quote prices; future fit tests the cross-modal distances against raw `dp` and quote features. **Falsifier:** the two feature families do not share a usable scale or distances add no conditional value. **Alternative:** a shared encoder learns this relation directly. Cost: low-to-moderate. The price-like semantics support plausibility, but exact encoding and units are undocumented.

## 9. State-dependent causal alignment of the second instrument

**Mechanism.** In a small latent pressure coordinate for each instrument, maintain past-only cross-covariance summaries over a bounded delay range. Use their stable peak to align the current `i0` state with the corresponding already-observed `i1` latent state, and pass peak strength and delay uncertainty. This re-indexes one instrument’s state; it does not append a generic bank of raw lag features or skip updates.

**Assumption / prediction.** If `i1` sometimes leads `i0`, the useful delay is locally persistent and asymmetric. Prediction: predictive contribution should peak at a reproducible causal delay and weaken when the alignment confidence is low. **Cheapest test:** no-fit synthetic streams with known delay verify that the estimate never uses future `i1`; future fit compares fixed alignment with adaptive alignment. **Falsifier:** estimated delay jitters or gains vanish under matched controls. **Alternative:** synchronous common factors or the moving-numeraire representation. Cost: moderate. Other high-frequency markets show asymmetric lead/lag patterns ([Huth & Abergel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.7103)), but that evidence is not about this anonymous pair and does not establish a stable delay here.

## 10. Convex-support state for excursions in common/relative space

**Mechanism.** After mapping the two instruments to a low-dimensional common-versus-relative plane, maintain the causal convex hull of a bounded recent window. Encode the current point’s signed distance to each hull face, whether it extends the hull, and the support direction of the extension. This state distinguishes “same current value inside the recent range” from “same value as a new directional extreme” without exposing individual lags.

**Assumption / prediction.** A breakout or return into a recent joint-state envelope may matter beyond current coordinates and marginal volatility. Prediction: examples at the same current point should receive different predictions depending on hull extension and support direction. **Cheapest test:** no-fit geometric toy paths with matched endpoint/interior values confirm the distinction; future fit tests hull features against current coordinates plus mean/scale summaries. **Falsifier:** hull state adds no held-out value or is dominated by the window’s most extreme noise. **Alternative:** a smooth recurrent state may already retain excursion context. Cost: moderate, with bounded-window hull maintenance and reset semantics. The market interpretation remains speculative.
