# Learning and information proposals — second pass

## Novelty audit and scope

The first-round weighted-MSE proposal restates the already-current objective; sequence normalization overlaps H-RR17-06; shared target coordinates overlap H-RR17-10; channel equivariance overlaps H-RR17-22; and the EMA innovation proposal overlaps the existing bank. I do not repeat them. First-round pooled-moment gradients, equal-target scalarization, unbiased importance sampling, Laguerre memory, and proxy orthogonalization remain possible distinct treatments, but are also omitted here. The prior portfolio and R2 critique already cover event clocks, cross-lag/path signatures, retrieval, hidden-state KD, ARTBP, calibration, group-DRO, future auxiliaries, and world models; those families are not relabeled below.

All sequences contain exactly 20,000 rows, so there is no long-sequence versus short-sequence imbalance in this task. Per-sequence clipped-label weight mass and validation scoring-mask counts may vary, but that is part of the observed evaluation measure; no sequence reweighting is proposed. These six cards are hypotheses only. No new data, code, fitting, or inference was used.

## 1. Remove only persistent cross-target gradient conflict

**Treatment.** Compute separate per-target gradients for the shared trunk. When their dot product is negative, project each gradient’s conflicting component off the other before averaging; leave target-specific heads untouched and do not rescale gradients merely because their norms differ. This is distinct from equal scalarization: the official two-target mean still defines the base objective, while projection changes the optimizer direction only in conflict cases. **Assumption.** Negative alignment is persistent across sequence batches and reflects representation interference, not minibatch noise. **Prediction.** Conflict frequency remains high across seeds, and projected updates improve both target losses more often than the unmodified mean gradient. **Cheapest check.** Verify projection algebra on synthetic 2-D gradients (no fit); later compare train-only gradient angles and paired endpoint WP. **Falsifier.** Conflict is rare or unstable, or the projection raises one target while reducing aggregate WP. **Alternative/cost.** The plain mean gradient is the exact descent direction for the scalarized objective and needs one combined update; projection needs separate gradients and may sacrifice that objective. PCGrad uses this projection in general multi-task settings ([NeurIPS 2020 paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/3fe78a8acf5fda99de95303940a2420c-Paper.pdf)); its results do not establish benefit for these two targets. This changes optimization, not available signal.

## 2. Precondition sequence-cluster gradient noise

**Treatment.** Estimate a small, damped low-rank covariance of shared-trunk gradients from independent sequence or sequence-block contributions, then precondition the optimizer step while leaving per-row loss weights and expected gradient unchanged. This differs from example importance sampling: it corrects correlated gradient directions after sampling rather than changing which rows are sampled. **Task-specific deficiency.** Adjacent observations within a 20,000-row sequence are dependent, while a diagonal adaptive optimizer sees coordinatewise scale and not cross-parameter gradient covariance. **Assumption.** The covariance of sequence-cluster gradient noise is stable enough to estimate and has harmful low-rank directions. **Prediction.** Lower update variance across sequence orderings at fixed exposure, potentially with fewer updates to the same WP. **Cheapest check.** Derive the effect on a two-dimensional synthetic gradient covariance (no fit); later compare preconditioned and ordinary updates using matched sequence blocks. **Falsifier.** Covariance directions vary by fold or WP falls after preconditioning. **Alternative/cost.** Adam or a larger batch is simpler; covariance sketches add memory, estimation, and matrix operations. This is an optimizer hypothesis, not new predictive information.

## 3. Model conditional label noise without changing the scored target

**Treatment.** For each target separately, fit a heteroscedastic observation model on `y=clip(t,−2,2)` under the metric’s label-tilted measure `w=|y|`: predict mean `μ(X)` and conditional variance `v(X)` with weighted Gaussian negative log likelihood. Under a correctly specified conditional mean/variance model, the population minimizer for `μ` remains `E_Q[Y|X]`; at inference only `μ` is returned, and `v` never gates predictions or changes official weights. **Task-specific deficiency.** The anonymous indicators may contain input-dependent measurement noise, but weighted MSE gives no diagnostic for separating residual dispersion from a learnable conditional mean. **Assumption.** Conditional variance is learnable and reasonably stable across sequences; it is not a proxy for omitted signal. **Prediction.** Variance calibration identifies noisy regions and may regularize finite-capacity mean fitting, without changing the target functional. **Cheapest check.** Verify the conditional mean identity on a small symbolic distribution (no fit); later assess cross-fitted variance calibration and paired WP. **Falsifier.** Variance fails to transfer or systematically labels predictable residuals as noise. **Alternative/cost.** Weighted MSE is simpler and already targets the correct mean; this adds a variance output and likelihood tuning. It models observed-label uncertainty, not an unobserved “true” market target.

## 4. Replay the true prefix before training on a chunk core

**Treatment.** If a later recipe trains on sequence chunks, choose a chunk start `t`, replay rows `0…t−1` from the real sequence reset to reconstruct the same forward hidden state that inference would have at `t`, then compute loss/gradients on the chunk core. Keep the replay prefix gradient-free and fixed-length chunk gradients; this is not a new truncation estimator or a state-memory feature. **Task-specific deficiency.** Arbitrary chunk starts initialized to zero make an interior training row appear to follow a sequence reset, unlike inference. The first 99 warm-up rows and full reset-local history define the actual forward-state distribution. **Assumption.** Chunk training is used and reset-at-chunk alters the state materially; full-sequence training already avoids the issue. **Prediction.** Chunked and full-prefix predictions agree at the core start, reducing boundary-conditioned loss spikes. **Cheapest check.** A deterministic toy recurrence can verify replay equality (no fit); later compare boundary/core losses. **Falsifier.** Existing training already carries/reconstructs state or replay parity does not affect WP. **Alternative/cost.** Full-sequence training is simpler conceptually but more expensive in memory; prefix replay adds forward compute. This fixes exposure consistency, not predictive signal.

## 5. Penalize unstable cross-family curvature, retain main effects

**Treatment.** Add a small penalty on mixed finite-difference curvature of each prediction with respect to broad input families—such as `i0` versus `i1`, or price-like versus volume-like channels—while leaving first-order responses unpenalized. Use stable feature identities; do not assign depth, side, or economic meanings to anonymous indices. **Task-specific deficiency.** A flexible model may fit brittle cross-family interactions among correlated channels that vary across sequences; an arbitrary adjacency graph is not supported by the schema. **Assumption.** Most transferable dependence is locally additive and the high-curvature interaction component is nuisance. **Prediction.** Lower prediction sensitivity and fold-to-fold variation under small in-distribution input perturbations, with similar or slightly lower train WP. **Cheapest check.** Show on a two-input toy that the penalty suppresses a spurious interaction while retaining additive effects (no fit); later compare regularized and unregularized models. **Falsifier.** Stable held-out WP depends on the penalized mixed interactions. **Alternative/cost.** An unregularized interaction model captures genuine nonlinear cross-impact more easily; finite-difference or Hessian penalties add computation and may create off-manifold artifacts. The penalty is a model-bias intervention, not an information source.

## 6. Gate score-gradient steps by denominator uncertainty

**Treatment.** Keep the exact pooled WP and its global moments as the target. From per-sequence additive contributions, estimate a leave-one-sequence-out jackknife uncertainty for each target’s weighted prediction variance denominator; scale the next optimizer step down only when the relative denominator uncertainty is high. Do not average sequence correlations, shrink the official evaluation denominator, or change target weights. **Task-specific deficiency.** Rare clipped-label weight mass can make a minibatch denominator estimate unstable even though the final score is globally pooled. There is no sequence-length imbalance, but effective weighted sample mass can differ by sequence. **Assumption.** Sequence blocks provide a useful uncertainty estimate, and denominator noise rather than genuine score curvature causes harmful steps. **Prediction.** Lower batch-composition sensitivity without changing the evaluation statistic. **Cheapest check.** Derive the jackknife rule for two synthetic sequence blocks (no fit); later compare step-direction variability with fixed-step training. **Falsifier.** Denominator uncertainty is negligible or damping slows learning and lowers WP. **Alternative/cost.** Exact full-population moments avoid this estimator uncertainty but require expensive full passes; jackknife tracking adds sequence-level state and may be noisy. This is optimizer control, not a new metric or signal.

## Scope note on clipping and sequence mass

The task has equal-length sequences and the official scorer deliberately gives each selected row weight `|clip(y,−2,2)|`. Therefore, “correcting” rare-label sequence influence would change the target population, while length correction is inapplicable. If a future trainer averages sequence-normalized losses, that would need an objective-alignment audit; it is not evidence that the current trainer does so and is not proposed here.
