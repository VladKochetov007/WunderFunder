# Independent blind critique — no-fit ideas

These are diagnostic hypotheses, not claims that any signal or failure is present. The no-fit checks below use only the published contract and algebra; the separately labeled future-fit checks would require authorized data analysis and fitting.

## 1. Is feature-index structure being invented?

**Question/change:** Audit every proposed feature transformation against a relabeling of the 112 input coordinates. Treat each coordinate as a stable identifier, but do not give numeric neighbors a shared physical meaning or treat indices as known book depth. **Why it matters:** A model can gain apparent structure from a false geometry, while complete permutation invariance can erase real coordinate-specific information. **Assumption:** Reusable roles may exist across channels, but are not specified by the index. **No-fit check:** Formally relabel all coordinates and their parameter references together; the represented function should be unchanged. Separately, note that the specification does not license a depth-based ordering. **Future-fit check:** Compare coordinate-specific and shared-role treatments under a fixed global relabeling protocol. **Falsifier:** Any supported ordering follows documented semantics rather than index. **Failure mode:** Mistaking relabeling consistency for evidence that channels are exchangeable.

## 2. Is the second instrument actually informative?

**Question/change:** Treat `i1` as an auxiliary source whose contribution must earn its place, with a route that can be removed cleanly from target-facing features. **Why it matters:** Only `i0` has targets; shared market language does not establish that `i1` leads, tracks, or improves prediction of either target. It may instead add regime noise. **Assumption:** Some conditional information about future `i0` movement may be present in `i1`. **No-fit check:** The target and input description is compatible both with an informative `i1` and with an independent `i1`; swapping instrument blocks is not an objective symmetry. Therefore the premise is unidentified from the contract. **Future-fit check:** A matched ablation would test incremental predictive value. **Falsifier:** Stable no-increment evidence across relevant evaluation units. **Failure mode:** Calling association or co-movement causal lead information, especially after selection on exposed validation.

## 3. Is the target observable at all from allowed history?

**Question/change:** Make target observability an explicit gate before attributing low performance to model capacity: document the target’s exact horizon, construction time, and any normalization if the official specification can provide them. **Why it matters:** “Future price movement” leaves many mappings possible, including horizons whose signal may be absent from current and earlier rows. **Assumption:** At least some target variation is conditionally predictable from available causal inputs. **No-fit check:** Compare the declared information set with the target description; the contract establishes causality but not horizon, so neither a useful lag nor an achievable ceiling follows. **Future-fit check:** Only after that ambiguity is resolved, use a preregistered lag/history ablation. **Falsifier:** The exact target construction shows no admissible input-time overlap. **Failure mode:** Treating a weak lag curve as proof of irreducible noise when preprocessing or target timing is wrong.

## 4. Can pooled score reward between-sequence coincidence?

**Question/change:** Decompose the official weighted covariance into within-sequence and between-sequence components as a diagnostic alongside the pooled score. **Why it matters:** The metric pools selected rows globally, not sequence scores. A predictor that tracks sequence-level offsets can score well even if its within-sequence movement signal is weak; a global result alone cannot tell these stories apart. **Assumption:** Sequences may differ in weighted target or prediction means. **No-fit check:** Derive the weighted covariance decomposition on paper, preserving each row’s `|clipped target|` weight; this establishes the possible confound but not its size. **Future-fit check:** Report the decomposition and sequence-block uncertainty without replacing the official metric. **Falsifier:** The between-sequence contribution is negligible in relevant evidence. **Failure mode:** Promoting per-sequence-centered correlation into a substitute leaderboard objective.

## 5. What can hidden score-row sampling identify?

**Question/change:** Keep prediction generation independent of score membership and assess whether conclusions survive plausible changes in which required rows are evaluated. **Why it matters:** Test `is_scored` is hidden, and every required row needs a prediction. A method cannot condition online on a mask it never receives; public validation-mask alignment alone does not reveal the hidden test sampling mechanism. **Assumption:** Selection might change which parts of the sequence distribution contribute to measured score. **No-fit check:** The API/spec makes score membership unavailable at inference, so any purported mask-aware online rule is impossible under this contract. No selection distribution is specified. **Future-fit check:** Re-score frozen predictions under declared alternative masks only if their provenance is known. **Falsifier:** The test selection design is documented as score-irrelevant for the compared claim. **Failure mode:** Treating a sensitivity mask as a forecast of hidden sampling.

## 6. Is normalization removing signal or nuisance?

**Question/change:** Frame common-level removal and relative/contrast features as competing hypotheses, rather than assuming price-like inputs are stationary coordinates with a disposable offset. **Why it matters:** The channel labels and target definition do not specify physical scale, stationarity, or whether absolute regime carries predictive information. A normalization can suppress nuisance and signal at once. **Assumption:** Some predictive information may reside in relative shape while some may depend on level or regime. **No-fit check:** Construct two mappings consistent with the published schema, one invariant to a shared offset and one that depends on it; the spec cannot distinguish them. **Future-fit check:** Compare matched raw, transformed, and paired pathways on the same split and ancestry. **Falsifier:** Only one representation survives clean comparisons. **Failure mode:** Inferring that an invariance is “financially natural” without evidence from this target.

## 7. Does within-sequence adaptation solve a real shift?

**Question/change:** Ask whether the past rows of a sequence reveal a changing input-to-target relationship that a fixed mapping misses; separate local-state adaptation from carrying information across sequences. **Why it matters:** Each 20,000-row sequence is independent, state resets, and only 99 warm-up rows precede the first prediction. Long history is available later, but the contract does not say the mapping drifts or that early context identifies the drift. **Assumption:** Within-sequence context may estimate a latent regime. **No-fit check:** The reset rule rules out cross-sequence memory, while no stationarity or drift claim is supplied; no benefit can be inferred from sequence length alone. **Future-fit check:** Compare declared short-context and adaptive-state treatments with per-time diagnostics. **Falsifier:** Adaptation adds no stable out-of-sample value. **Failure mode:** Using later evidence or hidden sequence identity to explain an effect unavailable to the causal state.

## 8. Is a shared two-target representation concealing a tradeoff?

**Question/change:** Track the two target correlations separately and test shared versus target-private signal paths as a mechanism question. **Why it matters:** The score averages two separately clipped, target-weighted correlations. A gain on one can offset a loss on the other, and the fact that both indicators concern `i0` does not imply they share a horizon or sufficient representation. **Assumption:** The targets may share some information and retain distinct information. **No-fit check:** Algebraically, the mean score does not identify per-target balance; the contract gives no exact target definitions that imply redundancy. **Future-fit check:** Compare matched shared/private treatments and report both target components plus the official mean. **Falsifier:** A shared path is consistently adequate for both. **Failure mode:** Selecting a model on the mean alone and silently accepting a material collapse on one target.

## Tempting invalid inferences

- Adjacent numeric feature indices are adjacent price levels, bid/ask order, or comparable physical quantities.
- `i1` is a lead instrument, or a symmetric copy of `i0`, because both use similarly named channels.
- The target horizon, units, volatility scaling, or maximum attainable correlation can be recovered from “future price movement.”
- High pooled WP proves within-sequence predictive skill, or an exposed validation score generalizes to the hidden score-row sample.
- `is_scored`, targets, or `seq_ix` can guide inference-time choices; sequence identifiers are ordered in time; state may cross sequence resets.
- A transformation that removes an apparent level or trend is safe because it seems scale-invariant, or a longer history must help because it is available.
- The two targets should share all predictive features, or their score components are interchangeable because the official result averages them.
- A no-fit contract audit or algebraic counterexample establishes which mechanism occurs in the real data; it only identifies what the supplied specification cannot settle.
