# Critique R2 — state geometry

I reviewed only the frozen `state_geometry.md` and the named prior cards. These are concise novelty and test-quality notes, not a veto on predictive experiments. Unknown semantics and scale are assumptions to test, not reasons to stop empirical work.

1. **Side-specific quote clouds.** The new treatment is an invariant set/measure summary that discards within-side channel identity; H-RR17-22 instead keeps component identity through an equivariant transform and identity escape. That is a real difference, but the no-fit permutation check is guaranteed by the construction and says nothing about which representation predicts. Assumption: within-side order is nuisance and the multiset retains useful information. Falsifier: a matched indexed or H-RR17-22 encoder reliably wins. Cost is moderate if several quantiles are emitted. Keep the loss of identity explicit.

2. **Volume composition plus total.** This is a new compositional chart relative to H-RR17-06’s prefix location/scale normalization. The log-ratio algebra only proves scale invariance; it cannot establish that these channels are positive compositions. Ratios plus total are essentially invertible on the positive domain, so their value is a factorized inductive bias, not new information. Assumption: common rescaling is a nuisance while allocation shape remains predictive. Falsifier: invalid/nonpositive domain or no matched benefit over raw volumes and raw-plus-chart. Cost is low, but zero handling changes the chart and must be fixed in advance.

3. **Quote shape with affine gauge.** The groupwise current-row chart is distinct in timing and grouping from H-RR17-06’s causal per-feature prefix statistics, but much of the treatment is a reparameterization: normalized residuals plus location and scale recover the original block when scale is nonzero. The algebraic affine-invariance check is correct but not informative about signal or learnability. Assumption: within-row relative shape is easier to learn than arbitrary level and units. Falsifier: a matched raw-input model is as good or better, or the shape branch contributes nothing after the explicit gauge coordinates. Cost is low. To claim invariance, the architecture or objective must constrain dependence on gauge coordinates; passing them freely does not.

4. **Bid/ask reflection parity.** This is a genuinely different, strong symmetry constraint from H-RR17-10’s common/contrast parameterization of the two targets. The involution/odd-head check verifies only the chosen algebra. The task contract does not establish that swapping sides and reflecting a price block maps either target to its negative, or specify how the eight extras transform. Assumption: a mirrored input corresponds to sign-reversed targets. Keep that as a testable prior: future paired augmentation or constrained-vs-unconstrained comparison can falsify it if mirrored responses are not odd or held-out WP falls. Cost is low-to-moderate; unknown physical scale makes the center choice material.

5. **Shared instrument chart, then common/differential latents.** The actual bias is shared encoder weights across i0/i1 before a role marker; the common/differential basis itself is invertible and adds no constraint when both coordinates pass freely. It is adjacent to H-RR17-22’s shared component processing, but its cross-instrument tying treatment is distinct if H-RR17-22 does not already tie those parameters. Role-exchange algebra is not evidence of task symmetry: only i0 is targeted. Assumption: low-level channel geometry transfers across instruments. Falsifier: matched shared and unshared charts, same core and capacity, with the shared chart losing on i0. Cost is moderate. Preserve the role distinction.

6. **Causal moving numeraire.** This is a meaningful extension of H-RR17-05: it estimates an online beta and residualizes i0 against i1, rather than gating a fixed lag basis or selecting an i1 expert. It is also distinct from H-RR17-10, which shares target outputs rather than input instruments. The toy causal-update check is plumbing, not evidence that beta is stable or useful. Assumption: price-like blocks share a comparable scale and i1 tracks part of i0’s local common component. Falsifier: unstable beta or no value against raw, fixed-beta, and no-i1 controls. Cost is low-to-moderate; define the bounded update and variance floor, then measure it. The exact target horizon is not needed for this predictive comparison.

7. **Local covariance geometry.** This overlaps H-RR17-11’s covariance orientation versus scalar activity, but extends it to whitened innovation direction/radius and eigenvalue summaries. That is an honest extension only if those radial/spectrum terms are the treatment under test; do not present “covariance state” as new. The affine Mahalanobis identity is a tautological no-fit check. Assumption: recent cross-channel covariance defines which innovations are ordinary. Falsifier: unstable orientation, redundancy with scalar/diagonal summaries, or no matched endpoint gain. Cost may exceed “moderate”: updating a d×d state is O(d²) per row and recomputing eigenvalues can be O(d³); specify the cadence or bounded summary and measure callback time.

8. **Trade prices located in the quote cloud.** A signed distance of dp channels from same-instrument p-channel distributions is a distinct cross-family relational feature, not covered by H-RR17-22 or H-RR17-06. But the affine-invariance check assumes the price and quote groups share units; it cannot establish that compatibility. Assumption: dp and p values live on a common meaningful scale. Falsifier: domain/scale incompatibility or no held-out value over raw dp and quote channels under matched capacity. Cost is low-to-moderate for a small set of robust distances. Do not infer best-quote semantics or order from channel indices.

9. **State-dependent causal alignment of i1.** This is a sharper extension of H-RR17-05’s fixed lag basis and H-RR17-09’s cross-lag comparison: estimate a locally changing delay and align latent states, rather than append raw lags. The synthetic known-delay test verifies the estimator, not that the real streams have a stable delay. Assumption: a causal delay persists long enough to estimate and helps beyond synchronous/fixed-lag fusion. Falsifier: delay jitter, phase-shift controls with similar results, or no gain over fixed-lag/no-i1 matched arms. Cost is moderate for a bounded lag window. Unknown target horizon limits economic interpretation, not a predictive test using only observed history.

10. **Convex-support state.** The treatment is a recent-window hull/excursion summary, distinct from H-RR17-23’s occupancy and transition topology; it encodes extremes and support direction, not code frequencies or transition counts. The toy-path check only proves two paths can share an endpoint and differ in hull state. Assumption: crossing or extending the recent joint envelope adds predictive context beyond current coordinates, mean, scale, and ordinary recurrence. Falsifier: no incremental held-out value over those matched summaries, or hull features simply reproduce recent min/max noise. Cost is moderate but underspecified: define the window, projection, forgetting rule, and online hull update before budgeting CPU time. Unknown market “breakout” semantics need not block the empirical comparison.

The strongest distinct treatments are #4 parity, #6 online residualization, #8 cross-family quote/trade distances, #9 adaptive latent alignment, and #10 hull state, provided their assumptions and nearest controls stay visible. #1, #2, #3, #5, and #7 are also testable, but their new content is respectively stronger permutation invariance, compositional factorization, current-row gauge reparameterization, cross-instrument weight tying, and an extension of covariance orientation. Most stated no-fit checks (permutation invariance, affine/Mahalanobis identities, odd-head algebra, synthetic delay, and matched-endpoint hull paths) verify the construction or implementation, not predictive information. The prior’s input-only covariance stability check is the most informative cheap screen among these, though it still cannot establish target value. None should be rejected solely because target horizon or physical semantics are unknown; each needs a matched empirical falsifier rather than a claim that its geometric story is already true.
