# Critique R3 — learning and information

Focused novelty and falsifiability notes on the frozen proposal file. Unknown target semantics are assumptions for prediction tests, not blockers.

1. **Metric-tilted conditional mean:** The derivation is useful, but the treatment duplicates the current weighted-MSE objective. Keep the identity as rationale, not a new card. A two-point toy check validates the derivation only; the meaningful falsifier is the proposed paired comparison if the current recipe does not already optimize this risk.

2. **Pooled-WP moment gradient:** This is a genuine optimization candidate: exact global-correlation gradients differ from minibatch correlation gradients and from weighted MSE. The finite-difference check confirms the formula, not that stale or estimated global moments help. State how moments refresh and handle clipping/saturated predictions; compare against both weighted MSE and minibatch-WP at matched compute. Keep the extra passes and global-moment noise in the cost.

3. **Equal target influence:** As written, this is not a separate mechanism from #2. Averaging the two exact per-target WP gradients with fixed 1/2 weights is just the gradient of the official mean score. It does not guarantee less target conflict: opposing shared-trunk gradients can still cancel, and equal scalar weights do not equalize gradient norms. Merge into #2; falsify the benefit by reporting both target deltas and whether the aggregate improves under a paired comparison.

4. **Objective-preserving importance sampling:** This is a distinct stochastic-optimization arm if inclusion probabilities and inverse-probability weights are exact. The toy two-stratum variance calculation is informative about the estimator, not this model’s gradient variance or wall-time benefit. Row sampling also needs a causal context/state policy for recurrent blocks; otherwise context-loading cost can erase savings. Compare corrected sampling with uniform sampling at equal wall time and verify the same expected training objective. Reject if correction variance or context overhead dominates.

5. **99-row warm-up normalization:** This is a narrow initialization variant of prior H-RR17-06’s sequence-local prefix normalization with a raw-level path, not a new family. Knowing there are 99 warm-up rows establishes availability, not stable scale estimates or nuisance shifts. Treat representativeness as an assumption; compare frozen warm-up statistics against raw/global baselines and falsify on instability or lower pooled WP. Do not wait for a physical interpretation of the channels.

6. **Laguerre memory basis:** This is a distinct temporal parameterization from H-RR17-09’s fixed cross-lag/path-interaction basis: a low-dimensional causal filter bank for smooth response histories. Synthetic impulse/decay tests can show approximation error over chosen poles, but the poles encode timescales, so “no target horizon” does not mean “no timescale prior.” Assume response is smooth enough; a matched fit against a free temporal learner falsifies it if sharp or irregular dependencies matter more. It is a representation/optimization constraint, not new input information.

7. **Proxy orthogonalization:** This could be a distinct predictive residualization treatment relative to H-RR17-05’s gated i1 expert, but the causal proxy-identification paper does not establish its assumptions here. The synthetic partialling-out identity checks algebra only. Cross-fit every nuisance projection, keep a direct i1 path, and compare joint, proxy-only, and residual-plus-proxy predictors. The claim is predictive, so no IV/rank condition is a stop gate; the falsifier is no incremental residual value or harm from removing collinear i0 information. Cost includes nuisance fits and extra inference branches.

8. **Shared predictive subspace:** This repeats H-RR17-10’s common/contrast target constraint in latent-factor language. It is a real inductive bias only if the shared subspace is rank-limited or otherwise constrained; an unrestricted shared component plus residual heads can be an invertible reparameterization. Unknown target horizons make sharing uncertain, not untestable. Compare constrained shared-plus-residual against independent heads and falsify if residuals dominate or the mean WP falls.

9. **Identity-tagged equivariance:** Direct repeat of H-RR17-22’s within-side equivariant encoder with identity escape. Moving values and tags together verifies permutation bookkeeping, not the benefit of parameter sharing. Only a matched comparison with a channel-specific map tests that claim; do not count this as a new mechanism.

10. **Slow baseline plus innovation:** A conventional EMA low/high decomposition with a raw-level bypass is an old slow/fast treatment, adjacent to prior prefix normalization and multirate memory. Offset cancellation is an algebraic property, not evidence of predictive value; the recurrent model already sees the raw current row and history. Fix the time constant before evaluation and compare raw-only, innovation-only, and both-path variants. The empirical falsifier is no gain or harm from the fixed decomposition, not unknown market semantics.

Only #2, #4, #6, and #7 presently look like distinct candidates, with #3 merged into #2. The remaining items are repetitions or implementation-specific refinements. None needs a new semantic prerequisite: state the assumption, preserve causal inputs, and use the matched empirical falsifier.
