# Mathematical scope and additional independent questions

No data arrays, model inference, gradients or fitting were used. The derivation below concerns the population problem, not finite-sample performance. The four proposals are discussion items and have no empirical support from this session.

## Weighted MSE is not intrinsically the wrong population target

For one clipped target Y in [-2,2], admissible history H, scoring indicator S, and W=S|Y| with 0<E[W]<infinity, define Q by dQ=W dP/E[W]. Where the conditional denominator is positive,

m(H)=E_P[WY|H]/E_P[W|H]=E_Q[Y|H].

For every square-integrable prediction f(H), E_Q[(Y-m(H))f(H)]=0. Thus Cov_Q(Y,f)=Cov_Q(m,f), and Cauchy–Schwarz gives Corr_Q(Y,f) <= sqrt(Var_Q(m)/Var_Q(Y)). A positive affine function of m attains this bound when the nondegenerate quantities exist. m itself is in [-2,2], so it is feasible under clipping. Weighted squared risk E_P[W(f-Y)^2] has minimizer m. Zero-weight histories and zero variances require their usual separate conventions.

This is our task-specific algebra, not a result quoted from a paper. It does not equate finite-capacity optimizers, batch-normalized losses, sampling distributions or the relative allocation of shared capacity to two targets. S is part of the theoretical evaluation distribution, never an online input; hidden test sampling remains unknown. It does not justify fitting a calibrator now. Conditional-mean/statistical scoring background: [Gneiting and Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437), inspected only at the abstract/overview level after an author-PDF retrieval failed.

Two consequences for ideation: proposing ordinary weighted MSE as a new method is a repeat, while a named finite-capacity/optimization/sampling failure is an actual hypothesis. Also E[sign(Y)|H] times E[|Y||H] is generally not E[Y|H], and neither is automatically the tilted m above. A direction/amplitude factorization needs a coherent joint objective or sufficient signed moments; an algebraic factorization alone creates no information.

## R1. Identify the target operator, not a particular historical model

**Change/question:** Compare a small prespecified family of descriptions of the anonymous target: fixed-row/event horizon displacement, first passage, forward extremum, or a difference of such summaries of supplied observable streams. Infer whether any family is compatible with the labels on training components, with scale/sign mappings and uncertainty explicit. This is a proposal for later permitted-data analysis, not performed here. **Why:** Different operators reward different memory and uncertainty summaries. **Assumption:** The label is related to a reconstructible functional of the provided future inputs; price-like channels might instead be transformed incompatibly or omit the construction signal. **Prediction:** One operator family has stable alignment across components, not merely best correlation after an unrestricted search. **Falsifier:** No stable candidate survives a declared search budget and component separation. **Control/cost:** Compare a common simple lag family with the richer operator; modest future data analysis, no fit needed for fixed formula agreement. Never feed actual future values or reconstructed labels online. Not prior label-clock reconstruction: that studied when labels changed, not which function of the future they encode.

## R2. Detect state aliasing before choosing more memory

**Change/question:** Compare matched current observations with increasingly long matched causal histories, asking whether target distributions distinguish those histories. Use an explicitly declared, target-free matching distance and source-qualified components. **Why:** If similar current inputs have different predictive futures but history separates them, information is hidden in context; if not, a larger recurrent state has weaker motivation. **Assumption:** Enough recurrent situations exist to make matching meaningful in112dimensions. **Prediction:** Adding specific history changes conditional target separation, not just sample count. **Falsifier:** Apparent separation disappears under activity/level/common-source controls or matched histories are too sparse. **Cost:** Future bounded descriptive analysis without fitting a model, with permutation/null controls. This is not a measured Bayes ceiling, a claim of deterministic labels, or proof that failure to match implies irreducible noise. It is a diagnostic research question, distinct from prescribing a backbone or ARTBP horizon.

## R3. Recurrent-state staleness across parameter updates

**Change:** Compare the carried hidden state computed before an optimizer update with a state re-burned under the current parameters on the same legal prefix, leaving the forward information set and gradient horizon controlled. **Why:** Training can carry h computed by theta_old into a chunk evaluated by theta_new; serving normally carries state under fixed parameters. **Assumption:** This mismatch is material at our update scale, rather than negligible at small learning rates. **Prediction:** State/forecast differences concentrate around updates and correlate with training instability; a bounded refresh treatment helps at matched compute. **Cheapest falsifier:** Later frozen-parameter replay measures old-state versus refreshed-state difference without optimizer steps; if negligible, do not fit the treatment. No replay was done now. **Alternative:** Longer credit assignment or ordinary gradient noise, not stale state. **Cost:** A bounded extra prefix pass or grouped updates, potentially expensive. [R2D2, ICLR2019](https://openreview.net/pdf/387fb2fcee8f74c53cf707a9856f40c458f33933.pdf) studies parameter lag/stale recurrent state in replay-based RL; its setting and claimed gains do not transfer here. The abstract was checked; direct PDF access returned browser verification. This is different from ARTBP, which changes the gradient estimator.

## R4. Directional conflict between the two target gradients

**Change:** Keep the same model and declared loss weights but compare ordinary shared updates with a treatment of genuinely opposing target-gradient components, such as PCGrad. **Why:** Equal target weights do not make two gradient directions compatible. **Assumption:** Conflict is persistent and damaging, rather than legitimate movement toward the joint optimum. **Prediction:** Any improvement should occur where conflict coexists with magnitude imbalance/curvature, not merely whenever a cosine is negative. **Cheapest falsifier:** Future bounded gradient diagnostics at fixed parameters, with no optimizer steps, can show whether the claimed condition is absent. None was run now. A later matched fit must compare official average WP and both target scores; no automatic guarantee of improvement. **Alternative:** Scalar loss normalization or separate capacity suffices. **Cost:** Separate target backward passes. [Yu et al., NeurIPS2020](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/3fe78a8acf5fda99de95303940a2420c-Paper.pdf), method/limitations inspected, explicitly does not establish that negative gradient cosine alone is harmful. Distinct from prior common/contrast architecture and equal scalarization.
