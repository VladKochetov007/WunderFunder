DESIGN (native/src/half.rs, rnn_fast.rs)
  - Weights: the I16 codes and row panels as before (|w| <= 32767, per-row scale). head.0 is split
    into its hidden and z column blocks, each with its own row scales.
  - Activations: int16 codes |q| <= 16383. Hidden states (GRU/LSTM, tanh-bounded) use the fixed scale
    1/16383 and are requantized once per step, right after each layer's update, then kept. z (GELU of
    the projection) uses its max |z| / 16383. Rounding is half-to-even after a clamp, which also maps
    NaN to -QX.
  - Kernel gemv_q16x: _mm256_madd_epi16; the int32 lane sums two chunks (32 columns) and is then
    flushed to fp32. Bound: 2 * 2 * 32767 * 16383 = 2,147,287,044 < 2^31-1, so no int32 overflow.
    fp32 lanes reduce in a fixed order, then y = sum * (row_scale * x_scale) + bias, with no FMA.
    gemv_q16x_portable does the same integer and fp32 operations in scalar Rust, so it returns the same
    bits. head.2 runs through a fixed-order 8-lane fp32 dot in q16x mode. Software prefetch is 4 KiB
    ahead (~2-5%).
  - Precision::Q16x falls back to Q16xPortable on CPUs without AVX2. It never falls back to fp32. The
    Python names are 'q16x' and 'q16x_portable'.

WHY NOT 2X (measured with native/examples/bench_engine.rs --kernel and the cycle counter)
  - L2-resident shapes: q16x is 1.65x (128w2) and 1.78x (128) faster than i16. The compute fix works.
  - GRU256: one member streams 2.2 MB of int16 weights per row. The kernel reaches 10.8-12 MAC/cyc
    from L2 (0.79 MB working set) but only 6.2 MAC/cyc = 12.4 B/cyc from L3/DRAM (3.1-6.3 MB), and
    that does not change with prefetch distance (0/2K/4K). So about 190 kcyc per member is a floor
    for 16-bit weights on this machine (other cores' jobs share the L3).
  - Narrower weights would get past that floor but fail parity on paper: 12-bit codes ~16x the weight
    error, int8 ~128x (the D256 i16 max abs is already 6e-5 against a 3e-4 gate).
  - Not tried, est. 15-25% for 1x and ~15% for 2x: keep ~1 MB of weights resident in L2 and stream the
    rest with non-temporal prefetch. The behaviour depends on the CPU, and the scorer CPU is unknown.
    Model-side levers: head.0 (512x512, 23% of the bytes) and the hidden width.
