2. THEORY / LITERATURE, FILTERED BY OUR REGIME (data-limited, noisy labels, small RNN, no instability)
T1 Noise regime: large-lr SGD noise regularizes and lr decay lets a net memorize noisy labels (Li, Wei, Ma 2019,
   arXiv 1907.04595); EMA of weights needs less lr decay and resists label noise (Morales-Brotons, Vogels, Hendrikx,
   TMLR 2024, arXiv 2411.18704). F3/F6 match both: no-EMA valid peaks at mid lr, EMA flattens the tail.
   Implication: spend extra passes at high lr with a longer EMA and a short cooldown, not in a long low-lr tail.
T2 Schedules: WSD with a short (1-sqrt) cooldown matches cosine at equal compute and can be continued (Hagele et al.
   2024, arXiv 2405.18392); no free gain at 4 epochs, but the right shape for 6 epochs (OneCycle-6 would again put
   ~25% of steps under 2% of the lr integral).
T3 Muon (orthogonalized Nesterov momentum, K. Jordan 2024; Liu et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.16982: weight decay plus
   update x 0.2 sqrt(max(A,B)) to match AdamW RMS; ~2x compute efficiency in compute-optimal LLM training) is
   steepest descent under the spectral norm: for a gate block G = U S V^T the step is -lr*0.2*sqrt(H) U V^T, so
   ||dW_hh||_2 = 3.2e-3 per step at H=256, while Adam's elementwise normalization turns a rank-1 gradient into a
   sign matrix with spectral norm up to lr*0.2*H (16x more). Exploding gradients need ||W_hh||_2 > 1/gamma
   (Pascanu et al. 2013), so Muon bounds the per-step change of exactly that quantity. But we have no instability
   (F1, F2), the speedup claim is for compute-limited training (we are noise-limited) and no RNN evidence exists
   (searched). Only an implicit-bias effect (spectrally even updates) can pay: prior 0, wide.
T4 Lion / Adamax / Shampoo / SOAP: same compute-regime argument, faster fitting = earlier memorization here. Wait
   for Muon's read before spending on another preconditioner.
T5 Adam beta2 0.999 (1,000-step memory) with beta1 cycled to 0.85: fine while nothing spikes (F1/F2). Lower beta2
   only helps against stale second moments after bursts; late clip rate 3% says bursts are rare.
T6 Clipping (Pascanu 2013; Zhang et al. 2020, arXiv 1905.11881: clipped GD = normalized GD under (L0,L1)
   smoothness). Ours is normalized GD in warmup (87% clipped) and a rare burst guard late (3%). Because the head
   is 90% of the norm (F2), the clip rescales the recurrent step by a head statistic; under Adam this only matters
   through step-to-step variation of the factor. Per-family clipping would be the principled variant; low value.
T7 TBPTT bias (Tallec & Ollivier 2017; Aicher, Foti, Fox, UAI 2020): truncation biases toward short dependencies,
   but gradients decay geometrically and the bias is small when the decay length << K. Ours: chunk 4000 -0.0031
   (E-083), 1000 +0.0005, 500 +0.0006 (E-123): longer truncation never helped, more optimizer steps did.
T8 Chrono init (Tallec & Ollivier, ICLR 2018, arXiv 1804.11188): b = log U[1, Tmax-1] spreads gate timescales
   log-uniformly in [2, Tmax]; GRU (h' = (1-z) n + z h): z = 1 - 1/T. Half the loss is in class-3 runs of 64-1024
   rows (E-323), but run-start information is ~0 there (corr 0.02) and since-features give elapsed time, and F7
   shows long-timescale units slow learning. LSTM is different: default f ~ 0.5 is a known handicap (Jozefowicz,
   Zaremba, Sutskever 2015: forget bias +1 closes the LSTM-GRU gap) and F4 shows our LSTM under-trained;
   --chrono-tmax 8 gives b_f in [0, 1.95] (mean ~1.3) = the +1 recipe with a spread. Biases stay fp32 in the engine.
T9 Orthogonal W_hh / layer norm: gates make orthogonal init secondary (free, bundled as chrono_orth); LN needs an
   engine port and per-row cost -> not proposed.
T10 EMA/SWA: E-332 soups (ep2+ep3) -0.0002: the EMA endpoint is already the best point of a OneCycle run. A longer
   horizon (0.9995 = 2,000 steps = 0.54 epoch) only makes sense when lr stays high (WSD).

3. SCALING (Q3)
- Kaplan et al. 2020 (arXiv 2001.08361): LSTMs match transformers on early tokens but plateau after ~100 tokens of
  context; loss exponent in params alpha_N ~ 0.076. Modern RNNs (Hawk/Griffin arXiv 2402.19427, xLSTM 2405.04517,
  RWKV, minGRU 2410.01201) follow transformer-like power laws, all in the data-rich, compute-limited regime.
- Data-constrained regime (Muennighoff et al. 2023, arXiv 2305.16264): up to ~4 epochs ~ fresh data, value decays
  after, excess parameters lose value faster than excess epochs. Our noisier labels shorten this: GRU128 optimum
  5-6 EMA epochs, GRU192 undistilled 2-3, GRU256 student +0.0005 in its 4th epoch.
- Distillation (Busbridge et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.08606): student loss is a power law in teacher cross-entropy with a
  capacity-gap regime; ours is weak-to-strong (0.26M teachers -> 0.98M student) and the student's gain is label
  denoising. Width pays only under distillation: 128 -> 256 (x3.8 params) = +0.0013 parent, +0.0018..0.0034 head.
- Prediction: ~+0.0007 local per doubling of student params (x0.8 public). GRU320 (1.51M) +0.0004 +- 0.0006;
  GRU384 (2.2M) ~+0.0006 but over the CPU budget. Data: +0.0025 per doubling (E-152) -> all-valid (+3%) ~+0.0001.
- CPU budget (E-336: GRU256 = 234 kcyc/row q16x, memory-bound on 2.2 MB int16 weights; 2 x GRU256 = 464 kcyc =
  57 public min > 52-min gate => gate ~423 kcyc/row). With cost ~ weight bytes: GRU320 (~3.0 MB) ~360 kcyc fits
  alone; LSTM192 (~1.4 MB) ~170 kcyc, so GRU256 + LSTM192 ~404 kcyc fits (fold pair GL vs single +0.0005 +-0.0005).
  The CPU-feasible upgrades are a wider single student (<= GRU320, needs an engine timing) or a GRU256 + LSTM192 bag.
