**Loss pre-mortem.** Write an expected quadratic objective over sampled rows as `a(f(X)-Y)^2 + b(f(X)-Q)^2`, where coefficients include sampling probabilities, mixture weights and each term's own normalizer. Expanding the quadratic at a fixed causal input `x` gives

`f*(x) = E[aY+bQ | X=x] / E[a+b | X=x]`,

when the denominator is positive and the prediction function is unrestricted. For chunk normalization, supervised `a` contains `w/D`, with `D=sum(w)` within the chunk; pooled normalization uses a common population denominator. The conditional weighted coefficients determine the optimum. Unconditional CV of `D` does not. A zero weighted residual within each denominator stratum can leave both gradients zero at the same prediction. Teacher coefficient changes can still matter even when the supervised optimum does not.

Fixture N1 has 16 chunks, each with one row at each of two inputs. Fourteen chunks assign weight 1 per row, and two assign weight 1000. Overall residual signs are balanced. In the harmless population the two large chunks have opposite residuals; in the harmful population both have positive residuals. The denominator multiset `[2]*14+[2000]*2`, target counts and CV are identical. The fixed conditional teacher is `Q(0)=-1/4`, `Q(1)=1/4`, mixed at coefficient 2/5 through its own unweighted average.

The harmless population yields identical normalized and pooled optima `(-2/5,2/5)`. In the harmful population normalized remains unchanged, while pooled becomes `(983/5035,5011/5035)`, a common shift of `2997/5035 = 0.5952333664`. These are exact rational solutions. Both prediction sets give target-weighted Pearson 0.7559289460 within floating precision. In this fixture the predictions remain within clipping bounds and have the same centered direction. A genuine surrogate optimum shift therefore need not improve the stated metric.

Attack a real objective report if denominator CV, mean loss, gradient size or convergence speed alone is presented as optimum bias. Also attack silent changes to membership, supervised/teacher relative coefficient mass, or sampling. The cheapest independent test is to evaluate both objectives at the same frozen checkpoint on exactly the same TRAIN chunks, retaining denominators, effective masses and signed gradient differences. Condition on the inputs used by the claim; a global average can conceal conditional changes. Unchanged free-function optima do not imply unchanged gradient variance or finite-GRU learning speed. Actual trainer weights, masks and teacher semantics remain unassessed in this blind pass.

**History-usage pre-mortem.** Fixture U1 first sets `H=C` and `Y=C`. Predictor `f(C,H)=C+10(H-C)` is perfect on support. Even clipping does not rescue the interpretation: this fixture's WP changes from +1 to -1 under the infeasible intervention.

In the second population, `C` and `H` vary independently and `Y=H`. Predictor `f(C,H)=C` has zero response to any history flip but MSE 2; a feasible history predictor achieves zero. Optimal current-only risk is 1, so the conditional history value is 1, not 2. The observed 2-to-0 improvement also fixes avoidable current-only model error. Thus neither large response nor zero response identifies missing useful information.

Recompute recurrent state from a perturbed prefix instead of reusing the original hidden state. Hidden-state zeroing can diagnose dependence while remaining unsupported as a market history.
