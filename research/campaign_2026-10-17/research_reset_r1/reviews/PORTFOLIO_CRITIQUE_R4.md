## Five portfolio-level failure points

1. **A target-derived clock is still being mistaken for an event law.** The full four-price-column census has 941 train and 312 valid need-eligible target changes outside the `i0_p0 AND i0_p11` condition; 92 valid exceptions are scored. The earlier 52 inspected exceptions are tick-sized, not float noise. That does not make existing event features useless, but it rules out freezing predictions, assuming target maturity, or gating output on that conjunction. Age since this conjunction is a left-censored input clock, not time since a target event. Keep every output defined on every required row.

2. **Several memory proposals are one crowded event/state family, and the new lag evidence is conditional.** The measured input events are persistent in the 64-sequence subset, which supports studying a compact causal memory state. But fixed-lag cross-instrument association varies by retrospective stratum: at validation lag 1, observed `i1`-lead phi minus its 1,000-row shifted control is roughly -0.027, -0.009, +0.036, and -0.134 in strata 0–3. Eight sequences per stratum, selected as the first eight row-group-sorted members of an already seeded sample, cannot support a universal coupling or serving gate. The broader 32-sequence-per-stratum EDA is still equal-stratum and cannot estimate natural pooled composition; its `i1_v*` train/valid histogram drift may reflect sequence heterogeneity. Train statistics use `need_prediction`, while valid scoring uses `need_prediction AND is_scored`. The full-vector no-adjacent-repeat finding covers only 128 train plus 128 valid sampled sequences and does not support row-compression claims elsewhere.

3. **The teacher/student bottleneck and information set are underspecified.** A large causal teacher (#02), hidden-state KD (#04), and distribution KD (#07) are related transfer routes, not independent architecture bets. Point-output KD can erase the state or uncertainty that made a teacher useful; hidden-state KD can overconstrain a smaller student. Card #24 is **Test-Time Training fast-weight sequence memory as a training-only teacher**, not a future-context/privileged-input teacher: its “future” refers to a later authorized research phase. It remains a distinct, causal within-sequence adaptation mechanism, with no target input or deployed student-side online gradient update; any such experiment waits for explicit future fit authority. For #18, future feature embeddings can serve as training-only contrastive targets, but must never enter the current-row inference path. #25’s imagined latents are distinct from real future inputs, but the student still has to act only on its current/past causal state.

4. **The proposed losses and output geometries do not automatically optimize the official metric.** The score is global pooled, separately clipped and weighted for each target, then averaged. The negative target correlation in retrospective strata does not establish that a copula, common/contrast basis, or joint distribution loss will help the score; an invertible linear output rotation adds no capacity to a full linear head, while a genuinely nonlinear head is a separate capacity/regularization hypothesis. Distributional ideas also have close negative precedent: the audited histogram-MSE and histogram-CE arms were negative on strict comparisons (histogram-CE's strict interval was wholly negative), and the E468 joint tilted code head was negative although its shared regression anchor also lost. A conditional joint summary could differ, but it must say which input-conditional information was absent from those arms. Positive affine rescaling does not change weighted correlation when clipping is inactive, so #20 matters only through clipping or a genuinely nonlinear correction and must be cross-fitted; older ridge corrections also had positive fit skill but negative held-out skill. Bounded DRO (#21) changes the training objective; group robustness is not the same quantity as the official pooled score.

5. **Novel architecture names are being counted as independent evidence.** Mamba3-MIMO (#14), xLSTM matrix memory (#15), and Griffin local attention (#16) are distinct operators but all face the same endpoint test: does a fixed-budget causal student improve the exact score over the incumbent? The local history contains a below-GRU GDN comparison, a negative accumulated-interval memory comparison, recipe-specific positive distillation results, and a volume-code probe that reversed from +0.0062 in-sample to -0.0078 under chain-half cross-fit. These findings neither rule out new mechanisms nor license a broad teacher sweep. Match compute, retain one student endpoint, and treat every gain as unmeasured until a later authorized fit.

## Deduplication map

- **Teacher transfer:** 02, 04, and 07 all ask what information transfers into a compact student. Register one causal, context-matched student endpoint first. Hidden-state and distribution targets are loss variants; do not stack every KD target and call the combined result a new architecture. #24 is related only at the broad student-transfer endpoint: its teacher has causal, within-sequence fast-weight updates after explicit future fit authorization, not privileged future inputs. Keep its adaptation mechanism distinct from output/hidden-state KD.
- **Event-triggered memory:** 03, 08, and 12 share the hypothesis that event timing should alter memory. Keep their operators distinct—soft writes, auxiliary renewal targets, and a change alarm—but use a shared fixed feature-event definition and shared no-fit control. Exclude extras; never use target changes to define the event vocabulary.
- **Cross-stream order:** 05 and 09 overlap as directed lag kernels / antisymmetric pair interactions. Let the fixed-lag basis be the control; retain a signature-area term only if it adds stable feature-only structure over that basis and a count/sum-matched or circular-shift control.
- **Slow joint state:** 11, 12, and 23 overlap around continuous second moments, alarmed state, and discrete transition topology. Compare each to a simple causal magnitude EWMA or occupancy/one-step-count baseline; the code-topology branch is not justified by the old volume-code probe unless it adds cycle structure beyond occupancy.
- **Architecture alternatives:** 14, 15, and 16 are mutually informative substitutes, not a combined teacher stack. 01 is the anchor; 02 is not a fourth architecture family. 19 is an optimization contrast and should not be entangled with architecture changes.
- **Pretraining / latent prediction:** 17, 18, and 25 are distinct objectives (masked reconstruction, contrastive future-feature target, imagined causal latent rollout), but all must demonstrate transfer to the actual student. #24 is a separate future-authorized fast-weight teacher procedure rather than a future-input objective. Do not pool their losses in the first test.
- **Target geometry / objective:** 07, 10, 20, and 21 can all alter output behavior or training emphasis, but are not equivalent: joint KD, nonlinear output basis, posthoc calibration, and group-robust loss. They require separate exact-metric ablations; no single “target geometry” result validates the others.
- **Retrieval vs fast weights:** 13 uses deterministic, non-gradient content lookup; #24 updates fast weights through causal within-sequence self-supervision, with an eventual fixed student. These are separate mechanisms despite both carrying sequence-local state. Keep retrieval low-priority and never share its cache across sequences.

## No-fit gates: what is required and what is not

**Required before any future fit or deployment work:** use one shared synthetic/structural harness to verify causal prefix invariance (changing a future suffix cannot alter earlier outputs), exact sequence reset, deterministic repeat calls, train/step/export parity, warm-up behavior, and output on every required row. A `seq_ix` may identify a reset boundary but is not a feature. No target or scoring mask may enter inference; future target values cannot be an inference feature or cross-sequence state. Ordinary target supervision on permitted training rows remains legal once training is authorized. For #06, preserve the raw path and verify prefix statistics are finite through warm-up; that checks implementation, not benefit. For #13/#23, test that the cache/code state clears at the boundary. For #14–16, one reset/chunk parity harness is enough; do not invent three expensive EDA gates.

**Useful only where a claim depends on an observable input mechanism:** use a predeclared event vocabulary and fixed lags for #03/#05/#08/#09, per-sequence summaries, both halves, and a phase-shift control. The existing control already supplies one such diagnostic on 64 sequences; repeating it over the remaining members of the seeded 32-per-stratum sample is the cheapest extension. It still cannot estimate forecast gain. #12 needs a different no-fit diagnostic: synthetic shifts with predeclared false-alarm and detection-delay summaries against an ordinary causal activity baseline, not a fixed event-lag test. #24 can receive a synthetic within-sequence update-order, reset, determinism, and runtime check, but no model fitting or market-score inference now. For #22, an input-only within-side symmetry check can reject an obviously unstable tying scheme, but it cannot prove label invariance; the identity escape is essential. For #23, compare transition topology against code occupancy and shuffled order, while remembering that the prior code probe reversed sign under chain-half evaluation.

**Not a necessary gate, and not a way to identify score gain:** no-fit statistics cannot choose among Mamba/xLSTM/Griffin, establish KD benefit, validate the student bottleneck, judge ARTBP, or show that an imagined world-model rollout helps. Toy tests can verify operator correctness and complexity only. Masked SSL/CPC feature reconstruction and future-feature contrastive targets can be checked for path leakage, but their representation utility requires a later authorized fit. #07/#10 need the actual conditional output comparison; the measured target correlation alone is not a gate. #20 can be algebraically inspected for clipping, but calibration gain requires out-of-fold predictions and labels. #21's group counts and training loss can be formed from training labels only once fit is authorized; no inference-time classifier is required. A label-derived training group is legal supervised loss bookkeeping if derived only from permitted training rows and kept out of the predictor. It is not legal as a validation/test gate, hidden-test class assumption, or per-row serving feature.

## Corrections to the earlier critiques

1. **Bid/ask side is known; physical depth order is not.** The official overview explicitly maps `p0..p10` and `v0..v10` to the bid side, and `p11..p21` / `v11..v21` to the ask side. It also says an index is an identifier, not a depth rank: `p0` need not be best bid and `p10` need not be deepest. Therefore the round-2 wording that bid/ask interpretation of the volume groups is wholly unsupported was too strong. Side-group pooling is legal and can be hypothesized; named queue position, best/deepest level, signed order flow, or aggressor meaning remains unsupported.

2. **Training-only label groups are not inference inputs.** R2 correctly says target-derived market classes cannot be serving gates and EDA's retrospective strata do not establish hidden-test mix. That warning should not be broadened into a ban on labels defining groups in a future training loss. After training is authorized, groups computed from permitted train targets may support a bounded-DRO/group objective; the predictor must still use only legal inputs, and group weights must be judged on chain-aware OOF/exact global WP. The current contract forbids fitting now, so this is a legality clarification, not authorization to train.

3. **The lag audit is a signal to retain a hypothesis, not to promote #05.** It shows positive event autocorrelation and short-lag asymmetry in the selected strata. But the sample is first-eight-by-row-group within an exposed, target-stratified sample; the cross-instrument effect changes sign/magnitude across strata; no targets were used in the audit; no forecast was measured. Don't treat the positive lag-1 differences as support for an inference-time label-class reliability gate.

The final 25-card portfolio should therefore preserve distinct mechanisms, explicitly mark prior and risk, and include a cheapest no-fit falsifier where a mechanism is testable from inputs. For every card involving forecast quality, keep gain **unidentified** until a later authorized, fixed, chain-aware comparison reports the official pooled clipped weighted-Pearson metric. No numerical public-gain estimate follows from this critique.
