# Six-scout synthesis and experiment frontier

2026-09-27, updated through E-226. This is a hypothesis ranking, not a claim of hidden-set performance. The user reported a measured public **0.6622** for the corrected pair, versus 0.6618 previously. Our earlier 0.6631–0.6634 forecast for that pair was high by 0.0009–0.0012; the new six-epoch packages have no public measurement. The latest supplied leader is 0.6677 and the user is 16th. E-224 found the five measured packages have the same reserved-holdout and public rank order, while local gain sizes transfer inconsistently. E-225 found the mixed pair's holdout gain is concentrated in class 3, with uncertain class-sliced estimates.

The immediate information gain is a public check of the prepared mixed six/four package: replacing the seed-3 four-epoch member by six epochs gained +0.001208±0.000512 in the matched pure-chain OOF pair, but the direct 368-sequence reserved-holdout gain over the submitted corrected pair was only +0.000653±0.000747 at λ=.02 and the previous public forecast was miscalibrated. Four of five daily slots remained in the user-supplied snapshot. The 0.0055 leader gap exceeds the incremental local gains here, so a later research cycle should emphasize new class-3 conditional information and period-transfer falsifiers before searching many more seeds.

## What previous winners suggest, with transfer limits

- [Optiver Trading at the Close first place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-trading-at-the-close/writeups/hyd-1st-place-solution) blended three **individually strong** model families. Its online retraining and cross-sectional features require data this scorer does not release; the transferable lesson is a strict strength-plus-diversity gate.
- [Optiver Realized Volatility first place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-realized-volatility-prediction/writeups/nyanp-1st-place-solution-nearest-neighbors) used recovered time order and neighbor aggregates. Future-neighbor features cannot run in our causal row stream. Its transferable lesson is validation by market period and stable local state, if the latter is observable from past rows.
- [Jane Street Market Prediction first place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/jane-street-market-prediction/writeups/cats-trading-yirun-s-solution-1st-place-training-s) used a supervised auxiliary representation and seed averaging. The nearest local analogue is the already successful next-change head plus independent EMA seeds; another head needs residual information after those.
- [Jane Street Real-Time Market Data Forecasting eighth place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/jane-street-real-time-market-data-forecasting/writeups/evgeniia-grigoreva-private-lb-8th-solution) gives a closer streaming GRU analogue: its author reports about +0.001 on its own CV and leaderboard from auxiliary responders at several future horizons, and checks a 200-day validation gap. Its much larger online-update effect used released lagged targets, which the Connectome API does not provide. A prior causal reconstruction recovered about 90.5% of matured class-3 targets, but online recalibration using them lost badly (E-080). Our 2/4-th *target-change* auxiliary test E-107 was flat (−0.0001/+0.0003) on an earlier recipe. Directly decoded components of class 3's four-*book-event* target law (E-079) remain distinct, but are low priority until a label diagnostic shows new information; no gain is claimed.
- [DRW Crypto Market Prediction first place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/drw-crypto-market-prediction/writeups/drw-solution-1st) used a strong neural predictor with a smaller tree contribution and purged time-group validation. That supports our strong-member gate and chain-grouped evaluation, not adding a weak tree for its own sake.
- [Ubiquant Market Prediction first place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ubiquant-market-prediction/writeups/k-i-y-1st-place-solution-our-betting-strategy) emphasized stable validation across time and capped model training; some winning time-id aggregate features would require future or cross-sequence test rows here. Our direct analogue is the period robustness audit plus late-epoch regularization.
- [G-Research's winner recap](https://www.gresearch.com/news/wrapping-up-the-g-research-crypto-forecasting-competition/) says its top three attributed more of their gains to feature engineering than model swaps. Here broad engineered features have already failed, so a feature needs a specific causal residual test before another neural training run.
- The [2012 Kaggle Algorithmic Trading Challenge winner paper](https://www.ms.k.u-tokyo.ac.jp/sugi/2013/Kaggle.pdf) reports that partitioning *future forecast horizons* gave the decisive edge, and averaging three models fitted on separate samples helped further. Our outputs are a fixed two-target future readout at every streamed row, so the direct horizon partition does not transfer. The data-subset ensemble echoes the local independent-seed gain; a chain-resampled training member is a specific untested variance/diversity analogue, subject to the two-member strength gate.
- A [previous Wunder Predictorium participant's retrospective](https://github.com/Vk2245/-Vk224-Wunder-Challenge-LOB-Predictorium) reports a large batch-to-streaming score collapse from full-sequence statistics. This is participant-reported, not an organizer-verified winning solution. It reinforces our local inference-parity gate; future-window and full-sequence normalizers remain inadmissible.
- A [prize-winning earlier WundeRNN participant](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/denisalpino_machinelearning-quantfinance-hft-activity-7408310006657519616-7_UT) describes relative-time attention, slow fusion and a small quantized ensemble, while also reporting seventh place on both boards. This is a self-reported architecture account, not a reproducible winner repository. Relative-time memory is a plausible model-family idea, but the current 20,000-row streaming problem and prior local larger-model failures put it behind the low-cost variance and scoring tests.
- [Mean Teacher](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01780) supports weight averaging and consistency as a way to reduce estimation noise. Our EMA-4 result is measured locally; a six-epoch continuation is an experiment, not a literature-guaranteed gain.
- The [mlfinlab sequential-bootstrap implementation](https://github.com/hudson-and-thames/mlfinlab/blob/master/mlfinlab/sampling/bootstrapping.py) provides a concrete way to count label-window overlap and sample uniqueness. Our class-3 episode factor is a cheaper bounded approximation adapted to this row stream; the repository does not establish that this factor improves weighted Pearson here.
- [Queue-reactive order books](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0563) and [queue imbalance](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03492) motivate event-clock, depletion-versus-refill signals. Their published results do not establish predictability in this anonymized dataset, so an episode-level residual screen comes first.
- [Ntakaris et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05384) jointly predict the next price-move direction and the number of events until it. Our current GRU already trains a next-change direction/time auxiliary head. The closest remaining transfer is to test whether that head adds a causal inference readout on long class-3 episodes; simply adding the same auxiliary task again would duplicate an existing gain.

## Ranked live hypotheses

1. **EMA for six epochs with since-weighting (variance).** Fixed final epoch 5 beat the four-epoch same-seed model on both folds, +0.0016 pooled (bootstrap SD 0.0010), and improved the independent seed-4 pair in both folds. Excluding 94 validation sequences connected to training chains leaves +0.00156 as a single and +0.00121±0.00051 as a partner at λ=.02. The pair lift is positive at λ=0 and .05, in six of eight row-group order bins, and after removing any one bin. Replicating six epochs in seed 4 was positive on both folds but added only +0.000388±0.000317 over the mixed pair at λ=.02, below the predeclared +0.0005 gate; thus the mixed pair remains ahead on accuracy evidence. Two final six-epoch seeds are already fitted as a faster hedge. Versus the corrected old pair, their full 368-sequence holdout delta is +0.00040±0.00111 at λ=.02; the 48 chain-isolated holdout sequences favor them by +0.00515±0.00468. Both results are uncertain, and the full holdout is an ancillary model-selection check rather than an independent test-set estimate.
2. **More weight on the validation regime during training (selection and variance; stopped).** Repeating the same eligible validation sequences twice in the EMA-4 since-weight pool added no unique data and about 12% optimizer steps. Fold 0 improved by +0.0020±0.0009 versus the obsolete four-epoch parent at λ=.02, but the pure-chain pair lift over the selected mixed six/four champion was only +0.000406±0.000929, below its +0.0005 gate. Fold 1 and final fit are not justified for this dose. An equal-step control would be required to attribute even the single-model gain to period weighting.
3. **Shallow strong member (variance, diversity and runtime; stopped).** The [Jane Street Real-Time author's solution](https://github.com/evgeniavolkova/kagglejanestreet/blob/master/solution.md) kept a one-layer GRU beside a deeper GRU. Our one-layer EMA+since candidate was competitive alone on fold 0 but gained only +0.000359±0.000522 at λ=0 and +0.000352±0.000482 at λ=.02 as a partner on 432 pure chains, below its predeclared +0.0005 paired gate (E-226). Stop before fold 1/final fit; the possible speed gain was not measured. The two-layer reference lacked since-weighting, so this was not a pure depth ablation.
4. **Decoded event-clock queue race (diversity plus new information; lower priority).** A small competing-up/down-risk model would use train-decoded refill/depletion intensities and the class-3 four-event target law. Raw 4/16-event volume-code displacement models failed a cheap event-level screen (E-215), so another model needs a better physical decoder and evidence of incremental direction information before any GPU fit. A full member additionally needs standalone WP within about 0.003 of the anchor and scorer runtime under 60 minutes.
5. **Direct event-component supervision (low-priority target structure).** E-079 approximated the class-3 target as a rounded mean across the next four book-price events. A head for directly decoded mid-price components at events 1, 2 and 4 would differ from E-107's target-change direction/time heads, but E-107's flat result lowers the prior. E-208's unused-head readout also failed. First measure distinct label information and decoding coverage; only then consider a fixed-weight fold-0 fit against the selected pair. No gain is claimed.
6. **Period-robust validation (decision quality).** Chain grouping still leaves 94/925 OOF validation sequences connected to training sequences. Pure-chain exclusion preserves the since-weight (+0.00086) and EMA-6 (+0.00156) gains. EMA-6 is positive in all three train/valid shift terciles and after removing any class or activity tercile, though these label-free bins are not true chronological periods. Its diagnostic future-distance slices are positive almost everywhere, including +0.0070±0.0031 in class-3 rows 33–128 rows before the next event; this future distance is never an inference feature. This changes confidence in candidates, not the score directly.

## Runtime and package gate

The [official submission guide](https://wundernn.io/connectome/docs/submission_guide) states a 60-minute, one-vCPU inference limit for 39.4 million test rows. The original two-GRU Python loop measured 105 µs per row while GPU training ran and projected to 69 minutes. A shared native feature update and AVX2/FMA dot product brought the verified two-six-epoch package to 65.9–78.2 µs per row on two scorer-image runs under GPU load, projecting to 43.3–51.4 minutes. Its 2.46 MB ZIP is `submissions/final_ema6_shared/final_ema6_shared.zip`, SHA-256 `5548c276ead87e01067e4750b662264d2276a2ef644a73edc40de8d378cb4cd4`. The mixed six/four package took 39.9 minutes **idle**, versus 53.5–62.6 minutes under active GPU training; the slow stress run exceeded the deadline but the idle run had substantial margin. The corrected old pair took 54.4–55.5 minutes under load. The v2 CPU fallback uses only seed 3 and projects to 35.9 minutes in its measured run. On 20 validation sequences the native two-six weighted Pearson differs from batch by −0.000056. These are local extrapolations, not organizer runtime or hidden-score results.

The user-reported `final_since2_gclp.zip` submission ran in **36m27s** with 619 MB and scored **0.6622** public. This actual duration is reassuring for the old package, but the two new shared-native packages still have only local timing projections. The mixed package's reserved 368-sequence holdout gain over the old corrected pair was +0.000653±0.000747 at λ=.02, too uncertain to convert into a public-score forecast after the old estimate overshot.

## Closed or low-priority after today's checks

- Chunk-500 and online-normalization partners: both beat the current seed pair on fold 0 and fail on fold 1 (E-185).
- Coarse causal mask-residual map: scored/unscored conditional residual contrast exists, but a cross-fitted correction loses 0.0013 pooled WP (E-186). AUC gains alone will not justify reopening it.
- Event-phase readout inspired by a prior algorithmic-trading winner: fixed bins and a shrunken residual intercept/slope improved only +0.00005 pooled WP across two chain folds (E-193), below its +0.0005 gate. The causal phase is likely already represented by the GRU. The original winner partitioned future forecast horizons, a different task from our causal elapsed-event bins.
- Fixed EMA checkpoint average over epochs 3/4/5: −0.0001 to −0.0002 pooled versus fixed epoch 5, and −0.00022/−0.00041 as seed-4 partner (E-196). Extra training helped; averaging these late checkpoints did not.
- The existing next-change direction auxiliary head added −0.00001/−0.00002 on pure-chain OOF folds as a shrunken residual readout (E-208), failing its predeclared +0.0005 gate. Reusing that head at inference is closed.
- Equal-target normalized MSE has little capacity-allocation rationale: the two targets account for 50.3% and 49.7% of weighted error mass on pure-chain OOF rows (E-204).
- A capped inverse-square-root class-3 target-episode training factor increased effective unique episodes by 23–31% in diagnostics but lost −0.00083 on fold 0 and −0.00093 on its pure-chain subset at λ=.02 (E-187/188/212). It failed the fixed first-fold gate; no second fold or final fit is justified for this implementation.
- Clipped-`|y|` weighting of EMA teacher consistency lost about −0.0001 to its four-epoch parent on fold 0 and −0.000624/−0.000788 to the selected mixed pair on 432 pure chains at λ=0/.02 (E-222). The predeclared +0.0005 gates failed; stop before fold 1.
- A first-99-row causal activity and early-prediction correction lost −0.00009 pooled on pure-chain OOF folds (E-211). The noncausal full-sequence mean oracle remains an upper bound, not a usable predictor.
- A bounded book-age blend gate between the two strong GRUs added only +0.00003 pooled on pure-chain OOF, far below its fixed +0.0005 gate (E-213).
- An i1 volume-code shock model over the last 4 and 16 i1 events reduced class-3 target-winner AUC by 0.032/0.036 on held-out long-interval midpoint samples, and its residual correlations were negative (E-214). Alongside the weak decoded i1 price lead (E-191), this makes a new cross-asset encoder a poor next use of GPU time. A better decoder could change that conclusion, but has not been demonstrated.
- The same 4/16-event shock design on own-book i0 volume codes lost 0.040/0.009 AUC on those samples (E-215). A properly decoded refill/depletion race remains distinct, but its raw-code shortcut did not earn a full member.
- Broad order-flow, larger recurrent cells, tree residuals, generic calibration, and uncalibrated teacher targets have already failed in E-088/089/092/123/160/174/181.
