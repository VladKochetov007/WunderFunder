# Metric, selection, shift, and overlapping targets — 2026-09-27

Scope: read-only review of the local scorer, training objective, research ledger, and primary sources. No training, leaderboard submissions, or participant repositories were used. Gains below are **prospective changes in pooled WP**, not measurements. Falsifiers use cached predictions and labels on held-out **chain groups**; they require no new model fit. The current public score is 0.6618 and the leader is 0.6651.

## Metric facts that constrain the search

The live [organizer data overview](https://wundernn.io/connectome/docs/data_overview) and [FAQ](https://wundernn.io/connectome/docs/faq) specify **one pooled weighted Pearson per target, averaged across targets**. The mask is `need_prediction & is_scored`; both target and prediction are clipped to ±2, and each target's weight is `|clip(y_k)|`. The test mask is hidden. Local `src/wnn/gmetric.py` follows this specification. The checked-in `METRIC.md`, `README.md`, and `utils.py` still describe a former per-sequence average; they must not guide model selection. E-161 already audited this difference. The official FAQ explicitly allows alternative validation masks and warns against relying only on the visible one.

For a causal information set X, clipped target Y, and scoring indicator S, the population predictor maximizing Pearson, up to a global affine transform, is

`p*(X) = E[S |Y| Y | X] / E[S |Y| | X]`.

If `S ⟂ Y | X`, this reduces to the minimizer of `|Y|`-weighted MSE. `src/wnn/nn.py:weighted_mse_loss` already clips Y and uses the correct per-target weight. E-160/E-161 correctly close a wholesale switch from weighted MSE to a different pointwise target. The finite-capacity and conditional-selection questions below remain open. A pooled Pearson score also contains between-regime covariance: E-060 estimated 18–20% of target variance between sequences. Local per-sequence correlations or class-only WP can therefore reverse the global ranking.

## Ranked hypotheses

### 1. The visible mask may select different *conditional outcomes* at the same causal state

**Mechanism.** `P(S=1|X)` weighting alone is insufficient if the scorer sees latent information about the next price-event outcome absent from X. E-103 found only +0.0022 AUC from adding labels to a mask classifier, but AUC tests classification of S, not the `|Y|`-weighted conditional moments that set `p*`. A small mask dependence concentrated on clipped, high-weight outcomes could move WP. E-087/E-122 show strongly structured selection; E-170 shows that a sharper **X-only** propensity no longer helps EMA-4. This is a distinct test of selection on residual outcomes. Related statistical framework: [Sugiyama et al., covariate shift and importance-weighted validation](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v8/sugiyama07a.html); its covariate-shift premise fails when `P(Y|X,S)` changes.

**Exact cheap falsifier.** On OOF validation predictions for the two EMA-4 members, fit separately in chain folds 0–1 and 2–3 a *low-dimensional* weighted residual correction using only causal variables already available at inference: `p0,p1`, class, `prop_v2`, `since_i0px`, and a3. In each training half, estimate the difference between scored and unscored `E[|Y|Y|X]/E[|Y||X]` in coarse predeclared bins; apply a shrunk correction to scored-row predictions in the opposite half, then reverse. Require positive **pooled** ΔWP on both half-swaps and at λ=0 and λ=.02, with chain bootstrap SE. Kill if the conditional-moment contrast is <0.05 target SD in all high-weight bins or the cross-fitted correction is ≤0 on either half. Do not use `is_scored` as an inference input.

**Likely gain / risk.** 0 to +0.002; only this card has plausible >+0.001 upside from a genuine optimum change. Main risk: unobserved test-mask shift, and noisy high-weight bins. **Overlap:** E-103 (label-aware AUC) weakens the prior; E-170 (better propensity), E-109/E-127/E-138 (simple calibration and gating) failed. A positive result must exceed those simple maps.

### 2. Validation may underweight the test period even after matching mask propensity

**Mechanism.** The organizer says the public and final sets are separate and the mask is hidden; there is no guarantee the joint distribution of market state and selected rows matches validation. E-161 found train/valid are mostly distinct time blocks of the same four target quanta, while E-094 found severe split detectability. Covariate density ratios could make a candidate that gains on validation lose on test, especially a mask-specialized LP-FT. A capped ratio can direct training capacity to later-period states, provided `P(Y|X)` is reasonably stable. [Sugiyama et al.](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v8/sugiyama07a.html) give the importance-weighted validation principle; [Li, Lam & Prusty](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/li20b.html) document the variance cost of raw density ratios. An [official Optiver winner writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-realized-volatility-prediction/writeups/nyanp-1st-place-solution-nearest-neighbors) is a useful precedent for recovering temporal order to make validation meaningful, not evidence that the same tactic will work here.

**Exact cheap falsifier.** Use `cache/seqstats_valid.parquet` plus cached per-sequence six WP sums, with no raw parquet scan. Form period groups using **only** activity and code-distribution summaries, never labels or `seq_ix`. In each of two held-out period splits, fit a train-period vs held-out-period discriminator and form clipped odds ratios in `[0.5,2]`; compare the ranking and ΔWP of the incumbent pair and available EMA/LP-FT/since-weight candidates under true-mask, λ=.02, and odds-weighted pooled six-sum scores. Kill if the capped ratios have effective sample size <50% of held-out chains, or if all candidate Δ rankings move <0.0005 and keep sign. If the validation prediction ranking changes >0.001 consistently, only then consider training with capped period weights.

**Likely gain / risk.** 0 to +0.002 by choosing or fitting a more transferable pair; no guaranteed direct gain. Main risk: period classifier detects harmless covariate movement or changes `P(Y|X)`, invalidating pure importance weighting. **Overlap:** E-094/E-161 establish shift; E-129/E-131 report blend sensitivity; prior per-class reweighting and segment routing failed (E-127/E-138). This proposal is period-based validation first, not another class gate.

### 3. Four-event label overlap makes row weights overcount a few future races

**Mechanism.** E-079 reconstructed class-3 `F` as the rounded mean of the next four i0 price events. Many consecutive rows therefore share the *same* future event quartet and much of the same target. Weighting all of them by `prop²·|Y|` may spend updates on repeated race outcomes; E-182's downweighting of old rows in a quiet stretch improved both EMA-4 folds by ~+0.0008, supporting this variance mechanism. An event-window *uniqueness* factor could retain the benefit more precisely, particularly where the first future event changes but the remaining three still overlap. Overlapping horizons reduce independent information in [Valkanov's Federal Reserve paper](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/ifdp/2006/853/revision/ifdp853r.htm); the exact event-window transfer here is an inference from E-079.

**Exact cheap falsifier.** On a capped sample of class-3 training and OOF validation sequences, use already decoded i0 price-event indices to assign each row its next-four-event interval. For each event, compute total `prop²·|clip(Y)|` exposure of rows whose label window contains it; let `u_i` be the inverse of the mean exposure over the four events, normalized to mean one *within class 3*. Compare `u_i` with the existing `since-weight(32,0.25)` and calculate the chain-level Kish effective number of event quartets and OOF residual covariance by `u_i` decile. Kill if `u_i` is >0.95 correlated with since-weight, raises effective independent-outcome count <10%, or residual concentration in the lowest-uniqueness quartile is absent. This is a **training-only** weight; future events never enter inference.

**Likely gain / risk.** 0 to +0.0015 beyond the current pair, likely nearer +0.0005; may have >+0.001 upside if the two members both benefit. Main risk: uniqueness weights suppress informative states the scorer selects. **Overlap:** E-182 and E-184 already validate an age proxy; E-174 self-distilled targets failed. This card changes multiplicity, not labels, and does not assume the self-teacher is calibrated.

### 4. Shared encoder capacity may be allocated to the wrong target despite a correct row loss

**Mechanism.** The score averages two correlations equally, while `weighted_mse_loss` divides the combined t0/t1 squared-error sum by their combined total `|Y|` weight. The two conditional optima are still correct, but a finite shared GRU may spend more capacity on whichever side supplies greater total weight and residual variance. `weighted_pooled_corr_loss` normalizes each target separately; E-171 found a small +0.0004 to +0.0005 for **LP-FT** with this objective. An equal-target, variance-normalized weighted MSE might deliver a larger main-fit gain without unstable batch correlation gradients. The official [global two-target metric](https://wundernn.io/connectome/docs/data_overview) is the reason to normalize per target.

**Exact cheap falsifier.** From cached EMA-4 OOF predictions, compute each target's global `W_k`, weighted residual MSE, weighted target variance, and six-sum WP. For each row calculate the first-order pooled-WP influence of a small move from `p_k` toward clipped `y_k` (differentiate `src/wnn/gmetric.py:wp_from_sums`), and compare the **target-level** total influence with the current wMSE gradient allocation. Kill if the two task shares differ <10% on both chain halves or if the under-allocated side is already the one with lower residual leverage. If it survives, compare equal-target normalized wMSE vs the same-seed EMA-4 parent on two held-out folds; the offline calculation is the cheap gate, not proof of gain.

**Likely gain / risk.** 0 to +0.001, with >+0.001 unlikely. Main risk: gradient share can differ without causing a performance bottleneck. **Overlap:** E-160 established the same per-target pointwise optimum; E-171 found only a small metric-aligned LP-FT gain. E-180 refuted *per-chunk total-weight* normalization; that is a different denominator from this target-balancing question.

### 5. Sequence-level means may be under-dispersed because training emphasizes repeated within-sequence rows

**Mechanism.** Pooled WP rewards both within-sequence changes and stable differences between regimes. E-060 says the latter contribute ~18–20% of target variance; E-160 measured an approximately 1.33 slope of sequence mean target on predicted mean and an oracle rescale gain ~+0.0018. A causal estimate of the sequence's regime from the 99 warm-up rows and an online activity EWMA might correct that compression. It must be trained and evaluated across chain groups; a realized full-sequence mean is forbidden at inference. [The official rules for independent sequences and causal state](https://wundernn.io/connectome/docs/data_overview) constrain the design. The [official Optiver winner writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-trading-at-the-close/writeups/hyd-1st-place-solution) is precedent for combining sequence models with online updates, not evidence that online label feedback exists here.

**Exact cheap falsifier.** On cached OOF pair predictions, compute a **causal** regime vector at rows 99, 999, 4999, and 19999 from warm-up/preceding inputs only: book and trade activity, target quantum/class, mean absolute price-code changes, and the GRU's running prediction mean. Fit a two-target ridge correction to the current prediction using scored rows of chain folds 0–1; evaluate pooled WP on folds 2–3, then reverse. Score λ=0 and .02. Kill if Δ≤0 on either swap or if >half the gain appears only after row 5000, indicating impractical slow adaptation. Do not use future rows, targets, or the hidden mask in the correction.

**Likely gain / risk.** 0 to +0.001; the +0.0018 figure is a **noncausal ceiling**, not a forecast. Main risk: the 99-row context is too short to infer the regime. **Overlap:** E-160 oracle slope, E-109 and E-161 simple calibration failures, and E-138 density gating. A positive result requires new causal sequence information rather than a static class or propensity scale.

## Decision

Prioritize cards 1 and 2 as offline audits because they are the only remaining routes here with a credible chance of **more than +0.001** through conditional selection or model choice under shift. Card 3 has the strongest local positive analogue (E-182) but may duplicate it. Cards 4–5 are cheap gates with low prior. Do **not** reopen per-sequence WP, plain score scaling, simple propensity gating, sharper propensity on EMA-4, per-chunk running normalization, or self-distilled target replacement: E-109/E-127/E-138/E-160/E-170/E-174/E-180 already constrain them. All proposed numbers need OOF chain-grouped evidence before a training run or submission decision.
