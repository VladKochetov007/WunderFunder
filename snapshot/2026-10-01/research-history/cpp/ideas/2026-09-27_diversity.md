# Diversity and compression under the streaming limit — 2026-09-27

**Decision:** If one new second member is built, make it an **event-clock, competing-risk book model** (rank 1). If the immediate priority is one-model inference, try **residual distillation of the two EMA-4 seeds** (rank 2). Both are research proposals, not measured gains. This report is based on the existing experiment log and primary sources; I ran no model checks, training, or submissions.

## Evidence and decision rule

The current anchor is an EMA-4 GRU. A different-seed EMA-4 partner beats the earlier GRU/baseline pair by about +0.0011 to +0.0013 on two chain folds (E-173, E-176–179); the 0.6618 leaderboard point is the earlier EMA-4 blend (E-165). The partner scan (`research/panel/partner_scan.md`, E-177) found prediction correlation near 0.995 for same-family GRUs. A correlated partner needs standalone weighted Pearson within roughly 0.003–0.004 of the anchor to have positive partial correlation. This is the **entry gate** for any full second member. Low residual-error correlation by itself is misleading: the GBM was different but too weak (E-093, E-115, E-177). Conv and LSTM variants did not survive the EMA-4 recipe (E-168, E-181).

Use the **pooled, clipped ±2, |y|-weighted Pearson** stipulated for this task, evaluated on held-out chain folds, with the same mask variants already used in `decide.py` (λ=0, 0.02, 0.05). Fit any mixing coefficient on other chains, then evaluate once on the held-out chains. Compare to the **current two-EMA-4-seed pair**, not to a single GRU or the older baseline blend. Keep the winner only if the sign is positive on both folds, the pooled gain exceeds its paired chain-bootstrap uncertainty, and projected inference stays inside the 60-minute CPU limit. The approximate gains below are priors for triage, not forecasts validated here.

Past competition results support *testing complementary representations at equal strength*, without assuming that another architecture will help here. The [Optiver Trading at the Close winner](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-trading-at-the-close/writeups/hyd-1st-place-solution) blended CatBoost, a temporal GRU, and a cross-stock transformer at validation-selected weights 0.5/0.3/0.2; all three models were individually competitive. The [Optiver Realized Volatility winner's ablation](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-realized-volatility-prediction/discussion/302626) reported private RMSPE 0.19699 for one LightGBM, 0.19644 for three CNNs, and 0.19545 for its heterogeneous ensemble. These are prior contests with different targets and runtime rules, so their scores establish a strategy to test, not an expected gain here. [Fort et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02757) give a separate reason to preserve independent seed trajectories: models sampled along one trajectory can be functionally much less diverse than independently initialized models.

## Ranked hypotheses

### 1. Event-clock competing-risk model as the second member — strongest structural bet

**Mechanism.** Keep a small causal state for decoded i0 best bid/ask price changes, signed depth/flow innovations, spread, elapsed real time (`a6`), and several exponential flow decays. Update the flow state on each row, but make the forecast head depend on **book-event state**: competing probabilities for the next upward/downward mid-price change plus no change over the next four book-price events. For class 3, turn the predicted event path into the expected rounded four-event midpoint target; for classes 0–2, use the same state with direct two-target heads because their exact target law has not been recovered. A tiny multinomial/ordinal head or shallow tree on the event state is a cheap first version; a small event-state recurrent net is the next version only if the first passes. Unlike the row-GRU, this represents the queue race and target clock explicitly. E-079 recovered the class-3 four-event rule; E-162 found first-move sign agrees with the class-3 winner on about 99% of rows. The source mechanism is grounded in [Huang, Lehalle and Rosenbaum's queue-reactive model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0563), [Gould and Bonart's queue-imbalance study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03492), and [Wu et al.'s queue-dependent Hawkes model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08938). Applying them to anonymized codes is our inference.

**Exact cheap falsifier, before a full fit.** On at most 32 held-out class-3 sequences per fold, read only causal decoded price events and existing cached GRU predictions. At each scored row, bin the *current* bid/ask queue proxy imbalance and the exponentially decayed signed book innovations into predeclared quintiles (bin edges from training chains). Report the next-move sign rate and the GRU's signed residual, weighted by clipped |y|, within each bin; then compute the pooled Pearson change from a cross-fitted, one-coefficient correction `GRU + beta * (bin-mean next-move sign - GRU direction)`, with beta fit on disjoint chains. Kill the event model if beta is nonpositive on either fold or the correction cannot add +0.0003 pooled. This read-only screening can use a bounded row sample, one CPU core, and under 2 GB; it does not justify a full member on its own. A later full member must pass the entry gate above.

**Likely gain / risk.** Plausible +0.0005 to +0.0015 **over the two-GRU pair** only if the book race contains conditional information the GRU misses. Main risks: coded volumes hide true queues (E-090), the label's first-move sign may already be saturated by the GRU, and class-3 gains may be diluted by classes 0–2. **Overlap:** tree B2/C2/2b and `src/wnn/markov.py` propose class-3 event or Markov models; this adds the competing-risk readout, global two-target entry gate, and a cheap residual falsifier. E-162 refuted a *time-to-event output rescaling*, not a directional queue-race model. E-088/089 refuted heuristic features added to the GRU, not an event-clock predictor.

### 2. Distill the *difference between two independent EMA-4 seeds* into one GRU plus a tiny adapter

**Mechanism.** Freeze the stronger EMA-4 member A. On chain-out-of-fold predictions from an independent EMA-4 member B, teach a small causal adapter to predict `delta = clip((A+B)/2) - clip(A)` from A's hidden state **and** a few raw/event-clock skip features. Inference is `clip(A + adapter)`; one GRU runs, while the adapter is only a small affine or two-layer head with a handful of exponential states. Match the **clipped ensemble prediction**, using the same |y|/mask weights as the score; regularize the adapter toward zero and include the actual label in a small secondary loss so a noisy teacher difference cannot dominate. The target is only the independent-seed correction, rather than a replacement of y by teacher predictions. The logic follows [Buciluă, Caruana and Niculescu-Mizil's model compression](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~caruana/compression.kdd06.pdf); [Menon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10419) explain the teacher-quality bias/variance tradeoff. Those papers do not guarantee that a small causal adapter can represent this correction.

**Exact cheap falsifier.** Use existing fold-0/1 A and B predictions. Form `delta` on scored and unscored rows, then a chain-disjoint ridge projection onto A's **cached hidden states if available** plus the existing causal feature cache; if hidden states are unavailable, use only the existing causal feature cache and treat a negative result as inconclusive. Use one fixed regularization chosen on a third chain partition. On held-out chains, require (i) positive |y|-weighted correlation between projected and true `delta` in both folds, (ii) a pooled score no worse than 0.0003 below the exact A+B pair, and (iii) projected per-row adapter cost below 10 µs. If (i) fails with hidden states available, stop; if (ii) fails, the runtime saving is unlikely to justify the loss. This is a later small CPU calculation, not a check run for this report.

**Likely gain / risk.** If it works, score is roughly pair score minus 0 to 0.0003 while saving about one GRU's inference work; any net gain comes from spending the freed runtime on a better anchor or extra cheap event branch. Primary risk is that B's independent representation is not recoverable from A's state. **Overlap:** prior 3-teacher target mixing helped an older single model (E-097) but added little to the stronger recipe (E-102/E-116), and same-time EMA-teacher targets lost 0.0031 (E-174). This proposal changes the target to the *paired-seed residual*, freezes A, uses cross-fitted B outputs, and explicitly measures recoverability before fitting a student. The earlier negative results make this conditional, not a default replacement.

### 3. Event-driven recurrent member with separate row and book clocks

**Mechanism.** Maintain a small recurrent state that changes only on decoded book/trade events, plus analytic decay of its state between events using `a6`/`a5`; feed a short event-history ring buffer to the output head at every required row. This is a different temporal inductive bias from the GRU that updates on every row. It can retain long class-3 context without 20,000 row-wise recurrent updates. The rationale comes from the event-time order-flow dependence in [Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08938) and the recurrence plus book representation in [DeepLOB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03668). The proposed sparse update scheme is our inference, not a result claimed by either paper.

**Exact cheap falsifier.** Before training, use existing causal event flags on a fixed 32-sequence/class validation sample to count updates and measure the fraction of |y|-weight on rows since the last event in bins 0, 1–8, 9–64, and >64. Compare the conditional GRU residual slope against elapsed event time in each bin, fitting slopes on one chain fold and checking on another. Kill if slopes change sign across folds or if the estimated event-state operations plus mandatory per-row output operations would save less than 25% of a GRU's CPU time. A full member later needs standalone WP within the E-177 entry gate; speed alone is insufficient.

**Likely gain / risk.** Speculative +0.0002 to +0.0010 over the pair, with possible runtime headroom if event updates are sparse. Risk is especially high because E-091 already shows clock information in inputs, E-162 found causal smoothers unhelpful, and the old architecture probe found all-column duplicate rows essentially absent (`research/lit/opus_arch.md` D6). **Overlap:** tree 2b and `research/ideas/r3_a.md` already propose event-time/dual-timescale GRUs. This version specifies decayed state during no-event rows and a runtime gate.

### 4. Class-3 exact-path branch as a cheap *correction*, not a class-specific blend weight

**Mechanism.** Use the existing decoded class-3 tick grammar to enumerate a small set of up/down/unchanged four-event paths. Estimate their probabilities from causal queue state, compute expected t0/t1 through the recovered target law, and add only the cross-fitted residual correction to the global two-GRU prediction. A finite path calculation may capture rounding and spread-state interactions that a smooth head underfits. The idea follows the queue-state transition framework of [Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0563); the four-event readout is project-specific E-079.

**Exact cheap falsifier.** On held-out class-3 chains, compare a predeclared 3-bin current-state path table (fit on disjoint chains) with the two-GRU prediction on |y|-weighted residuals. Score `clip(pair + beta * path_residual)` using one **global** beta fit on the opposite fold and zero correction in classes 0–2. Require a positive pooled delta of at least +0.0004 on each fold and stable cross-class output variance before considering a larger path model. A zero or opposite-sign delta kills it. The table is a small CPU fit for a later session; no training or evaluation was run here.

**Likely gain / risk.** +0 to +0.0008; cheap at inference if the table works. Risks are the 2.5% event-decoding errors in E-063 and concentration of any improvement in one class. **Overlap:** tree B2/4a and the Markov branch; E-127 refuted changing blend weights by class, so this tests a *calibrated additive prediction correction* with a single global beta instead. E-106 showed the GRU already tracks main symbolic effects, which is a strong reason to stop if the first table fails.

### 5. Shared-weight two-member GRU (BatchEnsemble) only as a compression fallback

**Mechanism.** A shared recurrent matrix with per-member rank-one factors may approximate two seed trajectories while reducing weights and some arithmetic; train or fit the factors against the existing two-seed teacher, preserving two hidden states but sharing the expensive input projection. [Wen, Tran and Ba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06715) report efficient BatchEnsemble inference in other tasks. A later [controlled study by Zamyatin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16936) found near-identical functions under shared rank-one factors, so genuine two-seed diversity is the central risk here.

**Exact cheap falsifier.** Without training, compare the two EMA-4 checkpoints' layerwise input/recurrent weight spectra and the prediction difference after a low-rank projection of each member's recurrent matrices (existing `research/lit/opus_arch.md` D3 already gives a rank-32 starting point). If a common rank-32 subspace cannot retain at least 99% of the two models' *prediction difference variance* on a fixed held-out sequence sample, do not build a shared-weight model. A positive result only permits a later fit and exact latency benchmark; it does not imply a score gain.

**Likely gain / risk.** Expected score gain near zero; potential 10–25% runtime reduction is speculative on one-core GEMV. **Overlap:** `research/lit/opus_arch.md` A10 already proposes BatchEnsemble, while E-177 and the 2026 study make it lower priority than ranks 1–2. A shared subspace could erase exactly the seed-specific correction that currently helps.

## Next decision

Run the **rank-1 residual screen** first, then the **rank-2 recoverability screen** when time for later checks is authorized. If rank 1 fails, retain the two independent EMA-4 seeds and prioritize improving both members' standalone quality; if rank 2 fails, avoid another generic distillation run. In every case, compare the final candidate against the current pair with clipped pooled WP and measured one-core streaming latency before replacing either member.
